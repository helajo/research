Scope & method

This note answers two questions: which of these 56 companies are financing the AI capex cycle most aggressively on their balance sheets, and — for the seven names where it matters most (the five U.S. hyperscalers plus two neoclouds) — how much financing sits off it, in leases, joint ventures, VIEs and guarantees that don't show up in a standard debt/equity ratio.

On-balance-sheet figures come from each company's latest reported balance sheet (Yahoo Finance, cross-checked against SEC EDGAR filing dates). Off-balance-sheet figures are read directly from the companies' own 10-K/10-Q/20-F text — not analyst estimates, not press reports. Two things to hold in mind throughout:

  • Different bases. On-balance-sheet debt is principal outstanding today. Off-balance-sheet lease commitments (Oracle's $260B) are undiscounted payments owed over the next 15–19 years. Guarantee/backstop figures (Meta, Alphabet) are maximum contingent exposure, not drawn debt. Adding them together produces a "total financing weight" figure, not a refinance-tomorrow leverage ratio.
  • Silence isn't innocence. A company showing no off-balance-sheet exposure here means none was disclosed under that heading in its own filing — not that no such arrangement exists anywhere in its structure.

Reading the table

  • Absolute debt is dominated by hyperscalers and legacy tech (Oracle $156B, Amazon $153B, Meta $84B, Broadcom $65B) — but Oracle's D/E of 3.67x stands well apart from Amazon/Meta/Broadcom's 0.4–0.8x range, marking it as the most debt-intensive of the group.
  • CoreWeave is the sharpest relative outlier: D/E of 8.94x on just $3.3B of equity, with 28% of its debt in capital leases — a neocloud built almost entirely on borrowed and leased capital.
  • Smaller power/crypto-adjacent names lever thin equity bases hard: TeraWulf (37.0x — near-zero equity), Vistra (3.9x), Bloom Energy (3.9x), Cipher Mining (3.4x).
  • Leasing carries the load at the infrastructure-heavy names: GE Vernova (72% of debt is leases), Amazon (57%), Meta (30%), Microsoft (29%) — signalling reliance on leased data-center and power equipment over bonded debt.
  • Dell, Core Scientific, Eos Energy and Cerebras carry negative equity, making D/E uninformative for them (flagged N/M) — a debt/assets or debt/EBITDA lens would read better there.

Benchmarks used: 5Y UST 4.421% · 10Y UST 4.716% · 30Y UST 5.243%. Spread = bond yield-to-worst less the nearest Treasury point.

What a CDS actually is, and why this section uses bonds instead

A credit default swap is not a loan — it's insurance on one. A protection buyer pays a running premium (the "spread," quoted in bps/year on a notional amount); if the issuer has a defined credit event (bankruptcy, missed payment, sometimes restructuring), the protection seller pays out. Nobody has to own the underlying bond to trade it — most CDS volume is hedging or speculation, sitting in a market entirely separate from the bonds themselves.

Nothing available in this workflow prices CDS directly (see §04's original sourcing note below). What this table shows instead is the bond market's own answer to the same question, via credit spread — a bond's yield minus a duration-matched Treasury yield. Different instrument, same underlying question: how risky does the market think this issuer is? The two normally move together (the "cash–CDS basis"), which is why the reported CDS levels mentioned in this section and the bond spreads in the table below tell the same story about Oracle, Meta, and the rest.

This is the payoff of the report's full chain of evidence: §01 shows the leverage sitting in the accounts, §02 shows what's hidden off them, §03 combines both into a truer picture, and this section asks whether the market has independently reached the same conclusion on its own. For Oracle, it has — and, per the live TRACE check further down, more emphatically than the underlying vendor data alone suggested.

摘要 · Summary

信用违约互换（CDS）本质上是对债券的"保险"，而非债券本身：保护买方定期支付保费（即"利差"，以基点/年计），若发行人发生约定的信用事件（破产、违约、有时含债务重组），保护卖方需赔付。交易双方均无需实际持有相关债券——CDS市场与现券市场是两个独立的市场。由于本工作流暂无直接获取CDS报价的渠道，本节改用债券信用利差（债券收益率减去同期限美债收益率）作为替代指标——工具不同，回答的问题相同：市场认为这家公司的信用风险有多大。两者通常同向变动（即"现券-CDS基差"），这也是本节新闻引用的CDS水平与下方债券利差能相互印证的原因。本节是全篇论证链条的落点：第01节展示账面杠杆，第02节揭示表外隐藏部分，第03节将两者合并为更真实的杠杆全貌，而本节则检验市场是否独立得出了同样的结论——就甲骨文而言，答案是肯定的，且下方FINRA实时验证显示，实际情况比初始数据更为严峻。

What the spreads say

  • Oracle is priced as a different credit entirely. At +160/+213/+261bps across the curve it sits 3–5x wide of the AA cohort — the only one of the group rated BBB−, with its long bonds yielding ~7.85%. The bond market has already repriced the leverage the §01 and §03 tables describe.
  • Meta's long end is the quiet divergence. Meta and Alphabet are one notch apart on rating, but at 30 years Meta pays +180bps against Alphabet's +107bps — a 73bp gap that isn't explained by the rating. Meta's curve steepens sharply past 10 years, which is where its $46B JV exposure and 2050s-dated lease obligations live.
  • CoreWeave trades as distressed-adjacent high yield. Its 9% 2031s yield ~12% at 89 cents on the dollar — +755bps, rated single-B, with no issues past 2032. It cannot term out its debt the way the hyperscalers can, which is the practical risk its 8.94x D/E implies.
  • Microsoft and Amazon are the cheapest borrowers — consistent with §02, where neither showed material off-balance-sheet datacenter vehicles.
  • Cross-check against CDS. Reported 5yr CDS levels (Oracle ~200bps, Meta ~93bps, Nvidia ~78bps as of Aug 2026) rank-order the same way as these cash spreads and sit modestly wider, which is the normal CDS-cash basis. The two measures agree on the story.

Sourcing note, and why this isn't TRACE

FINRA TRACE is free but not open: individual bond lookup requires accepting the Fixed Income Data User Agreement. That acceptance and the four live prints above were pulled directly by the report's requester through FINRA's own tool — genuine regulator-tape data, not a vendor feed. The base curve tables elsewhere in this section still come from secondary-market quotes aggregated by TradingView; expect ±10–20bps of noise versus a duration-matched OAS there, and treat single-bond readings as indicative: Amazon's 2031s alone quote across a 16bp range, which is why that cell shows a band.

  • Nebius carries no usable credit signal. Its six listed instruments are convertible notes trading at 141–433% of par — deep in the money, priced off the equity, not the credit. There is no straight-debt curve to read.
  • Microsoft's 5Y cell is a maturity mismatch, not a true zero: its nearest issue matures Sept 2030 (4.1yr) and yields 4.37%, marginally through the 5Y Treasury. Read it as "inside 25bps," not as negative.
