1. The Verdict

Not overpriced on the earnings you can see — but priced for a supercycle that must last through 2027, which is exactly what a memory-cycle peak always looks like.

On forward P/Es (Samsung 4.5x, SK Hynix 4.8x, Micron 6.6x) the trio looks absurdly cheap. But memory stocks are always cheapest on P/E at the top, because the "E" is peak-cycle. The honest gauge is price-to-sales: MU trades at ~12x sales and SK Hynix ~11x — roughly 3–4x their previous cycle-peak levels — and MU's market cap is ~$1.1T versus a prior-cycle peak near $150B. That premium is only justified if HBM's contracted, de-commoditized economics persist. The structural case is real (HBM sold out through 2026, shortage forecast into 2027, rebalance ~2028), so this is late-cycle, not bubble-top delusion — but the easy money has been made, positioning is unanimous (zero sell ratings), and the market will start discounting the 2028 supply rebalance sometime in 2027. Risk-reward now favors holding/trimming into strength rather than initiating new size. Among the three: Samsung is the value/defensive pick, SK Hynix the quality-but-purest-cycle-risk, Micron in between with the strongest near-term EPS ramp.

3. What Just Happened: the July Correction WEB

  • July 2: SK Hynix fell ~14.6% and Samsung ~9% in one session as a Wall Street chip rout spread to Asia — severe enough to trigger an emergency Kospi trading halt. Samsung + SK Hynix now make up roughly half the Kospi's weight (vs. ~a quarter at end-2024), so their swings are now index events.
  • Late June: the Kospi had already slid ~4.6% off its record (June 23) on a Broadcom-linked AI selloff; MU, SanDisk and other DRAM names fell sharply from record highs in sympathy.
  • June 30: Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron were hit with a US price-fixing class action over the memory shortage — a headline risk that echoes the 2002/2018 DRAM antitrust cycles.
  • Catalyst ahead: SK Hynix is due to list on Nasdaq on July 10 — a US listing that could unlock index/retail demand (and is one reason the pre-listing shakeout drew attention).
  • Commentary consensus: this looks like fear and positioning, not a demand break — AI memory remains in genuine short supply.

4. Where We Are in the Cycle WEB

  • Pricing is going vertical: DRAM contract prices jumped ~90% q/q in Q1 2026; a standard 64GB server RDIMM went from $450 (Q4 2025) to over $900 in 90 days. Hyperscalers locked in ~70% of bits.
  • HBM4 is the engine: Samsung and SK Hynix are both tapped as HBM4 suppliers for NVIDIA's Rubin platform; HBM capacity is sold out for 2026, with full-year volume and pricing effectively locked.
  • Duration forecasts: TrendForce/UBS-style models see HBM shortage persisting through 2027 (supply +15–17% vs. demand +20–22%), with rebalance around 2028 as all three ramp fabs. Some researchers already flag HBM price correction risk after 2026 as competition and capacity expand.
  • The structural argument: HBM is sold via annual pre-negotiated contracts — de-commoditized, more like a design-win business than spot DRAM. This is the core reason bulls argue "this cycle is different."
  • The cyclical counter: every memory upcycle in history ended with a supply response the market saw coming ~4 quarters before earnings peaked. If rebalance lands 2028, the equity peak is likely a 2027 event — and stocks discount it early.
中文摘要 · 周期位置 价格正在垂直上涨：2026年一季度DRAM合约价环比涨约90%，64GB服务器内存条90天内从450美元涨到900美元以上；HBM4产能已全部售罄至2026年底，量价锁定。行业预测短缺延续到2027年（供给+15–17% vs 需求+20–22%），约2028年供需再平衡。多头的核心论据：HBM按年度预签合同销售，已"去商品化"。空头的反驳：历史上每轮存储上行周期，股价都在盈利见顶前约四个季度提前见顶——若2028年再平衡，股价顶点大概率出现在2027年，而市场会更早开始定价。

5. Cheap P/E or Classic Memory Trap?

The bull math (Micron as the example): quarterly EPS is ramping violently — $4.78 (Dec-25) → $12.20 (Mar-26) → $25.11 reported Jun-24 (beat by 21%) → $31.28 estimated for Sep-26. Eight straight quarterly beats. Street consensus implies ~$150 EPS for FY27, putting the stock at 6.6x forward. Analyst mean price target is $1,486 (+51%), median $1,550; 40 of 45 ratings are buys, zero sells; a wave of targets went to $2,000 (Cantor, Barclays, DA Davidson, Susquehanna) after the June print. SK Hynix and Samsung trade even cheaper on forward earnings.

The bear math: valuation multiples that don't depend on peak earnings are at unprecedented levels:

Reconciling the two: the trio is not expensive if FY27 earnings are a new mid-cycle baseline (the HBM-contract argument). It is very expensive if FY27 is the peak of a still-cyclical industry that has merely gained a structural HBM kicker. Our read: the truth is in between — HBM genuinely raises the floor, but conventional DRAM/NAND (still the majority of Samsung's and a large share of MU/Hynix bits) remains cyclical, and today's 70–80% margins contain a large shortage-scarcity rent that will compress when capacity lands 2027–28.

6. Name by Name

SK Hynix — highest quality, purest cycle risk

The HBM leader (~55–60% share, first to HBM4 for Rubin) with a staggering 71.5% operating margin and +198% revenue growth. But it is the most memory-pure of the three: when the cycle turns, there is nothing else in the P&L. P/S ~11x is the red flag; forward P/E 4.8x is the siren song. Near-term: the Nasdaq listing (July 10) could add a US-demand kicker; the −29% pullback into it improves entry. Beta 2.0 — size accordingly.

Micron — the US-listed expression, strongest EPS ramp

Eight consecutive beats, EPS quintupling year over year, HBM sold out, and the only US-listed pure play (index/mandate flows help). At $985 (−22% off high) with a $1,486 mean target, the street sees +51%. But at 12x sales and a $1.1T cap, it is priced as if memory has permanently escaped its history. If you own it from lower levels, the June 25 target-raise frenzy was a gift for trimming; new money is buying the seventh inning.

Samsung — the value/defensive route

At 4.9x sales, 4.5x forward earnings, 0.47% yield and beta 1.5, Samsung is the cheapest and least cycle-pure: memory sits inside a conglomerate with foundry, mobile, and display. It lagged HBM3E but is qualified into HBM4 for Rubin — the catch-up optionality is the bull case, the conglomerate discount and foundry losses the drag. If you want memory exposure added today, Samsung offers the best downside math; it is also the one that rallied "only" 4.8x off its low.

7. What Would Change the Answer — Signals to Watch

  1. 2027 HBM contract negotiations (Q3–Q4 2026) — the single most important data point. Flat-to-up 2027 HBM pricing validates the "new baseline" thesis; price concessions start the clock on the top.
  2. Capex announcements — all three are ramping; watch for the quarter when combined bit-supply growth guidance crosses demand growth (~2027 per current forecasts).
  3. Samsung HBM4 qualification/share wins at NVIDIA — good for Samsung, margin-negative for the industry (triopoly discipline weakens).
  4. Hyperscaler capex guidance (Apr–May and Jul–Aug 2026 prints) — memory is the highest-beta casualty of any AI capex digestion.
  5. The price-fixing class action — low probability of near-term impact, but it caps how aggressively the trio can be seen to coordinate pricing.
  6. SK Hynix Nasdaq debut (Jul 10) — strong debut = more upside chase; weak debut = a tell on marginal-buyer exhaustion.
