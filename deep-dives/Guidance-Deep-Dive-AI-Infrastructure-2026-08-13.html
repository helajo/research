本报告覆盖过去 45 天内公布财报的 40 家 AI 基础设施相关公司（芯片、云计算巨头、电力能源、网络设备、数据中心）。核心发现：本季度最戏剧性的案例是四大云计算巨头对"资本开支"这同一个杠杆做出了完全相反的市场反应——谷歌和 Meta 因上调资本开支指引而下跌，亚马逊上调资本开支指引却大涨，微软下调资本开支指引同样大涨。这说明"资本开支指引方向"本身并不能预测股价反应，真正重要的是这笔钱背后的叙事是否具有说服力。

1. The Puzzle

We screened 52 companies spanning the AI infrastructure buildout: chip designers and equipment makers, the four hyperscalers, nuclear and grid power names newly repriced on AI electricity demand, optical/networking infrastructure, and crypto-miners-turned-AI-compute names. Of those, 40 reported within the trailing 45 days — a live, current snapshot of how this specific earnings season is being priced.

Across that set, EPS surprise size doesn't reliably predict the reaction. This quarter's real story is capital expenditure — who's spending more on AI infrastructure, who's spending less, and how the market is pricing each decision very differently depending on the narrative wrapped around it.

MECHANISM A
The Hyperscaler Capex Divergence: Same Lever, Opposite Outcomes

All four hyperscalers reported large headline EPS surprises this quarter and made a major capex disclosure. The stock reactions split exactly down the middle. Worth being precise about why the EPS numbers themselves aren't the explanation: three of the four headline EPS figures are substantially accounting noise, not operating performance, once you look at what's actually inside them.

Before the case studies — what the EPS numbers actually contain: Alphabet's reported EPS of $9.11 (a "+214% surprise") includes a $98-99B unrealized/pre-tax gain on its equity stakes in Anthropic and SpaceX, adding roughly $6.26/share — core operating income grew a real but far less dramatic 30% to $40.8B. Amazon's reported EPS of $5.75 (a "+215% surprise") similarly includes a $53.4B pre-tax unrealized gain on its own Anthropic stake; strip that out and underlying EPS was roughly $1.19 — which would have MISSED the $1.82 consensus, not beaten it. Meta's reported EPS of $6.18 (a "-14% miss") reflects $2.4B in legal charges and $1.2B in severance, both one-time; add those back and adjusted EPS comes to roughly $7.35 — which would have BEATEN the ~$7.14-7.22 estimate, not missed it. Microsoft's beat ($4.74 vs. $4.24 est.) is the one clean, organic number of the four. So for three of four hyperscalers, the headline "beat" or "miss" you'd read off a data feed is close to fiction. That's precisely why capex and growth-trajectory framing did the explanatory work that EPS surprise normally would.

MECHANISM D
For Early-Stage Infra Plays, EPS Is Nearly Meaningless

Three power/energy names built around long-duration AI electricity demand all posted large EPS "misses" by the conventional metric, and none of them fell on the print itself. But "guidance was fine" undersells what was actually happening in each case — there's a specific, different catalyst behind each one, and in two of the three the full story includes a real fight over whether the reaction was justified at all.

+14.8%

Pre-revenue nuclear SMR developer. EPS missed by a lot (-$0.28 vs. -$0.16 est.) — but revenue beat wildly ($1.21M vs. $0.13M est., its first-ever quarterly revenue), 2026 cash flow guidance was raised ($120-150M from $80-100M operating cash flow), and the company hit a reactor "criticality" milestone at its Groves reactor. The loss-per-share number really is close to noise here; but the operational proof points that replaced it as the market's focus didn't fully convince the analyst community either.

Coverage at the time flagged the muted reaction as arguably too small given the news, attributing it to sector fatigue: Vistra had already fallen roughly 11.5% over the prior month (matches the -11.5% anticipation drift below) as the broader "power for AI" trade cooled off, and a good quarter plus a big new partnership was only enough to stop the bleeding, not reverse it.

All three of these would have looked like "big misses, muted or positive reaction" mismatches if you only checked EPS surprise %. But the fuller picture isn't just "check EBITDA/cash flow instead of EPS" — it's that each had its own distinct, checkable catalyst (a specific named $1B partnership for VST, a specific regional power-market dynamic for TLN, an operational milestone for OKLO), and two of the three faced real, immediate pushback from analysts on whether the reaction was earned at all. All three also show up with a meaningful negative anticipation drift below (-14.4% OKLO, -11.5% VST, -10.3% TLN) — this whole trio had sold off hard in the weeks before reporting, the same broad AI-infrastructure de-risking wave that hit the hyperscalers in Section 2A, which is worth keeping in mind before reading too much conviction into modest single-digit pops off a depressed base.

3. Restated Takeaway

EPS surprise size is a weak signal on its own, and this quarter it's weaker than usual for a specific, checkable reason: for three of the four hyperscalers, the headline number was substantially accounting noise — equity-stake revaluations at Google and Amazon, one-time legal and severance charges at Meta — not a clean read on operating performance. Once that noise is stripped out, capital expenditure and how it's framed did the explanatory work that EPS surprise normally would: the same capex-raise decision produced a -7% reaction (Google, open-ended spend, no offsetting adjusted-EPS story), a -8% reaction (Meta, despite what would have been an adjusted beat), and a +15% reaction (Amazon, despite what was likely an adjusted miss) depending on whether the market believed the spend was disciplined and demand-constrained or open-ended and margin-eroding. Beneath that, a whole tier of companies (Oklo, Vistra, Talen) show that EPS surprise is sometimes simply the wrong metric to be checking at all — revenue, EBITDA, cash flow, and physical capacity guidance are what these business models actually run on. And two mechanically interesting cases (Bloom Energy's idiosyncratic short-seller overhang, Core Scientific's GAAP/non-GAAP gap and one-day-delayed reaction) are reminders that even a full mechanism taxonomy won't catch everything on the first read — some stories only resolve with a specific piece of context that a spreadsheet alone won't surface, and checking what's actually inside a reported EPS number before trusting it is worth doing every time, not just when the result looks surprising.

EPS 超预期幅度本身是弱信号，而本季度尤其如此——四大云计算巨头中有三家的 EPS 头条数字很大程度上是会计噪音：谷歌和亚马逊来自股权投资重估，Meta 来自一次性法律及遣散费用，并非真实经营表现的干净反映。剔除这些噪音后，资本开支及其背后的叙事承担了本该由 EPS 超预期承担的解释责任：同样是"上调资本开支指引"，谷歌因缺乏对冲的正面叙事、支出没有上限而下跌 7%；Meta 即便剔除一次性费用后本应是超预期，仍因资本开支侵蚀现金流而下跌 8%；亚马逊剔除重估收益后很可能是"未达预期"，却因 AWS 加速增长和资本开支被定性为"产能不足"而大涨 15%。再往下一层，Oklo、Vistra、Talen 这类公司说明，对某些商业模式而言，EPS 超预期从一开始就不是该看的指标——营收、EBITDA、现金流和实体产能指引才是真正驱动估值的东西。而 Bloom Energy 的做空报告干扰和 Core Scientific 的 GAAP/非 GAAP 口径差异及延迟一天的股价反应，则提醒我们：在信任任何一个 EPS 数字之前，都应该先看清它里面到底装的是什么，而不只是在结果"看起来意外"时才这样做。
