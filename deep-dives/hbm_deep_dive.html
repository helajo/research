SECTION 2 · 第二节
What Is HBM? A Plain-English Explanation
什么是HBM？通俗解释

HBM stands for High Bandwidth Memory. It is a special type of RAM chip that sits physically on the same chip package as a GPU or AI accelerator, connected by thousands of tiny wires called through-silicon vias (TSVs). The "high bandwidth" means it can move data extremely fast — much faster than the regular memory in your laptop or phone.

🏭 The Factory Analogy
Think of an AI chip as a giant factory with thousands of workers (the GPU cores) who can process calculations simultaneously. Those workers are useless if they're sitting idle waiting for raw materials. HBM is the conveyor belt system that feeds each worker the exact data they need at exactly the right moment — thousands of times per second. The faster and wider the conveyor, the more productive the entire factory. Without HBM, your $40,000 Nvidia H100 is a very expensive paperweight.
中文摘要
HBM（高带宽内存）是一种特殊的RAM芯片，与GPU封装在同一个芯片上，通过数千条微型导线相连。可以把AI芯片比作一个拥有数千名工人的工厂，HBM就是传送带系统，每秒数千次地为每个工人提供他们需要的数据。没有HBM，一块价值4万美元的英伟达H100只是一块昂贵的镇纸。

Why isn't it the same as optics? Optics (the fibre and laser-based links that companies like Lumentum and Ciena make) is the highway between cities — it moves data between servers, racks, and data centers. HBM is the fuel injector inside the engine. Both matter, but they solve different problems at different scales. Patel's argument is that the most acute, least-substitutable bottleneck right now is inside the chip package — the speed at which the GPU can feed itself data — not the highways between buildings.

Who they are: Samsung is the world's largest memory company by revenue — and also makes smartphones, TVs, home appliances, displays, and foundry chips. This breadth is both strength and weakness: the HBM upside is diluted by exposure to consumer electronics, which is suffering from the very memory price surge that makes HBM so profitable.

The recovery story: Samsung badly fumbled the HBM3e generation — Nvidia famously rejected their chips in quality testing in 2024 due to heat and power consumption issues. Why the "conglomerate discount" matters: Samsung's semiconductor division (DS) is posting extraordinary results — operating profit increased nearly 48-fold versus Q1 2025. But the smartphone division (MX) and TV division are actually being hurt by high memory prices, which raise their own component costs. This offsetting effect suppresses the overall stock multiple relative to a pure-play memory company like SK Hynix. You get a discount on the HBM story — but you also get exposure to businesses that are struggling.

The labour strike risk: In May 2026, Samsung is facing a significant labour dispute. Its union is demanding 15% of operating profit in bonuses. After paying no bonuses in 2024 (during the memory downturn), workers are pushing hard. JPMorgan estimates that meeting full union demands could reduce 2026 operating profit by 7–12%, before any lost production from strikes.

Market trend: The memory upcycle is well underway, but the duration appears unusually long. Goldman Sachs called the 2026 supply-demand gap the worst in 15 years. SK Group's chairman expects the wafer shortage to persist until 2030. The reason is structural: new fabs take 4–5 years to build, and demand is compounding with every AI product cycle. This is not a typical 12-month memory boom.

Where we are in the cycle: HBM4 is just beginning to ramp. Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform (which uses 8 stacks of HBM4 per chip) is launching H2 2026. HBM5 is in development. Each new GPU generation requires more HBM than the last. The next 2–3 years of product cycles are essentially locked in — all three manufacturers are already receiving orders and design specifications from hyperscalers for hardware that hasn't been built yet.

SECTION 6 · 第六节
HBM vs Optics Stocks: Same AI Wave, Very Different Investments
HBM与光学股票对比：同一波AI浪潮，截然不同的投资

If you've been following the AI infrastructure trade, you've probably heard about optical networking stocks alongside HBM. Both sectors are riding the same AI data center spending boom. Both are supply-constrained. Both have produced spectacular returns in 2026. But they are solving fundamentally different problems — at different points in the stack — and understanding the distinction matters enormously for how you size and time these positions.

中文摘要
如果你一直在关注AI基础设施投资，你可能同时听说过HBM内存和光学网络股票。两个板块都受益于同一波AI数据中心支出热潮，都面临供应紧张，2026年也都创造了惊人回报。但它们解决的是根本不同的问题——在技术栈的不同位置——理解这一差异对于如何配置和择时至关重要。
FIRST: WHAT OPTICS ACTUALLY DOES · 首先：光学技术的实际用途
🌆 The City Infrastructure Analogy
Imagine an AI data center as a city.

HBM is the plumbing inside each building — the pipes that carry water from the mains to every tap on every floor. Without it, nothing in the building works, no matter how good the city's roads are.

Optical networking is the roads, highways, and bridges between buildings — it moves data from one server to another, from one rack to another, from one data center to another. As AI clusters scale from hundreds to hundreds of thousands of GPUs working in parallel, you need faster and faster roads between them.

Both are essential. But they operate at different scales, serve different engineering problems, and have different competitive moats.
中文摘要
把AI数据中心想象成一座城市：HBM是每栋建筑内部的管道系统——将水从总管道输送到每个楼层的每个水龙头；光学网络是建筑之间的道路、高速公路和桥梁——在服务器之间、机架之间、数据中心之间传输数据。随着AI集群从数百个扩展到数十万个并行工作的GPU，建筑之间需要越来越快的"道路"。两者都不可或缺，但它们服务于不同层面的工程问题，拥有不同的竞争壁垒。

What they make: Lumentum makes laser chips and optical components that go inside the networking equipment connecting AI servers. What they make: Coherent sits at the intersection of compound semiconductors, photonics, and optical networking. They make 800G and 1.6T optical transceivers — the components that move data between servers at the speed of light — as well as the underlying photonic materials (indium phosphide, or InP) that make those transceivers work. ⭐ The Plain-English Verdict: Which Should You Own? · 通俗结论：应该选哪个？

If you want the Patel-framework pick — own HBM, specifically Micron (MU). HBM is the constraint that binds first, has the fewest competitors, is hardest to replicate, and yet trades at the cheapest valuation relative to growth of any AI infrastructure category. A PEG of 0.08 versus a sector average of 0.67 tells you the market is still not fully pricing in HBM's earnings power. SK Hynix is the even higher-conviction version if you can access KRX.

If you want optics exposure — Coherent (COHR) is the most balanced pick. It has genuine vertical integration moats, a reasonable PEG of 1.7× (unlike LITE's nearly infinite trailing multiple), is S&P 500-listed with strong institutional coverage, and its earnings are set to compound at 45% CAGR to FY2028. It is the "SK Hynix equivalent" of the optics space — not the most exciting momentum story, but the one with the most defensible business.

LITE (Lumentum) at 261× P/E is a bet that everything goes right for a very long time. The growth is real. The technology lead is real. But after a 1,269% one-year run, you are paying for years of future success upfront. For new investors entering today, the risk/reward is much less asymmetric than it was twelve months ago.

AAOI is for sophisticated investors who can stomach 20–30% drawdowns. The story is compelling — US-based, Amazon-anchored, ramping fast — but not yet profitable, and the P/S above 30× means there is almost no margin of safety. It can double from here or fall 40%. Not a first position for a retail investor.
