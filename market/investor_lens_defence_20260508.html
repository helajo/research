The Fortune cover story this week is the most detailed public statement yet from the Anduril founding team on why legacy primes are structurally failing. Luckey and Schimpf are explicit: Lockheed Martin, RTX, Boeing, and Northrop Grumman are too slow, too expensive, and too reliant on cost-plus contracts and lobbying moats. Anduril beat Boeing, Lockheed, and Northrop for the Air Force's Collaborative Combat Aircraft programme; it won a $20B Army contract in March; it just secured a $100M Space Force SDA mesh networking modification (May 5–7); and its systems are actively deployed in the Iran war. The Tomahawk production stat — a decade of output consumed in three days — is the sharpest articulation of the procurement model failure Luckey has argued since 2017. It now has a real-world timestamp. Separately, Anduril's Altius drone struggled in Ukraine (Schimpf acknowledged this directly), providing a candid data point that Luckey rarely softens: real combat is the only valid test, and iteration beats perfect engineering.

↑ PLTR ↑ RKLB ↑ LHX (long-dated catalyst — 12–24 months) ↓ LMT ↓ RTX ↓ NOC ↓ BA

Pentagon proposes 240× increase in autonomous warfare budget: DAWG jumps from $226M to $54.6B for FY2027 — the single largest autonomous systems commitment in history
The Defence Autonomous Warfare Group (DAWG), created to unify the DoD's unmanned systems procurement, saw its proposed budget soar from $225.9M in FY2026 to $54.6B requested for FY2027 — a 24,300% increase. This is the most significant single validation of the Luckey/Stephens autonomy thesis in the history of US defence budgeting. The DAWG target: 200,000+ autonomous systems procured by 2027. The budget sits within Trump's broader $1.5T defence request, which also includes a $42B general increase for autonomous systems. The critical detail that mainstream defence coverage is missing: the DAWG budget is specifically structured to bypass cost-plus contract vehicles — it is designed to flow to production-ready autonomous systems companies using fixed-price, outcome-based procurement. This is exactly the contract model Luckey has argued for since Anduril's founding. Legacy primes — LMT, RTX, NOC, GD, BA — are not set up to compete on fixed-price autonomous systems at scale; their infrastructure, workforce, and incentive structures are calibrated for cost-plus. The DAWG creates a parallel procurement lane that systematically advantages Anduril, Shield AI, Joby Defence, and similar "neopromes." For listed companies, PLTR (software layer), RKLB (launch and space surveillance), and L3Harris (electronics) are better positioned than the traditional primes to capture DAWG-adjacent contracts.
Implications for listed tickers
↑ PLTR ↑ RKLB ↑ LHX — long-dated catalyst 12–24 months ↓ LMT ↓ RTX ↓ NOC ↓ GD ↓ BA
The DAWG budget jump represents the structural shift Luckey has argued was coming for a decade — it is now a line item. Legacy prime underperformance relative to S&P 500 is the trade; PLTR as govtech software proxy is the bull.

📊 Analyst Analysis · Not attributed to monitored investors
Pentagon awards $500M to Scale AI and classified AI contracts to seven companies — Anthropic excluded, SpaceX included (May 1–6)
Two significant AI contract events this week. First: the Pentagon awarded Scale AI (private, Meta-backed) a $500M contract to accelerate military AI adoption — a 5× increase from the prior $100M agreement. The award covers computer vision, generative AI decision support, and data operations; it is explicitly designed to speed AI adoption and bypass slow traditional procurement cycles. Second and more signal-rich: on May 1, the DoD awarded classified-network AI agreements to seven companies — Amazon Web Services, Google, Microsoft, NVIDIA, OpenAI, SpaceX, and Reflection AI. Notably, Anthropic was the only frontier AI lab excluded; the exclusion follows a supply-chain risk designation Secretary Hegseth formalised in March. The Luckey/Stephens read on this: the DoD is selecting on "dual-use credibility and mission alignment," not just AI benchmark performance. SpaceX's inclusion over Anthropic signals that the Pentagon trusts operators with national-security track records over safety-focused AI labs. The listed-company implications: NVDA (directly awarded a classified slot), MSFT (Azure for classified), GOOGL (classified cloud). Palantir is not named in the seven, but its Gotham platform is the software layer that runs on top of the classified infrastructure these contracts enable.
Implications for listed tickers
↑ NVDA ↑ MSFT ↑ PLTR ↑ GOOGL
Scale AI's $500M award is private-company signal, but confirms the govtech software revenue opportunity Stephens has described. The classified seven-company deal validates NVDA's position as irreplaceable inference hardware for national-security AI.
