Market commentary 市场综述

Monday's session (May 4) was dominated by a single macro shock: the UAE's air-defence systems intercepting 19 Iranian missiles and drones — the first activation of the UAE's missile shield since the US-Iran ceasefire began roughly a month ago. WTI crude surged 4.39% to $106.42 and Brent jumped 5.8% to $114.44, sending the Dow down 557 points (−1.13%) as energy-importing sectors — airlines, consumer discretionary, industrials — sold off hard. Home Depot lost 3.5%, Nike −2.95%, and Boeing −2.64%. The S&P 500's narrower −0.41% loss and Nasdaq's −0.19% dip reflect tech resilience, anchored by Palantir surging 1.4% after a blowout earnings report: 85% revenue growth, US revenue up 104%, and a raised full-year guidance to $7.65B. The VIX rose 7.65% to ~18.29 — elevated but not yet at stress territory (30+). Broad earnings season remains historically strong: 84% of S&P 500 reporters have beaten estimates, with aggregate EPS growth running at 27.8% vs the 13.7% originally expected.

#1
The UAE confirmed its missile defence systems intercepted 12 ballistic missiles, 3 cruise missiles, and 4 UAVs launched from Iran on May 4 — the first time UAE's shield has been activated since the US-Iran ceasefire was struck approximately one month ago. A drone also caused a fire at the Fujairah oil port on the UAE's eastern coast, a key petroleum export hub. This is the first direct Iranian strike on UAE soil in the current conflict and represents a significant escalation. The Strait of Hormuz — through which approximately 20 million barrels of oil per day (about 20% of global supply) transit — has already been partially disrupted since the conflict began, but Monday's events signal the ceasefire may be effectively dead. Iran's Foreign Ministry called US operations in the region a "ceasefire violation," suggesting both sides are framing the other as the aggressor. Oil markets reacted instantly: WTI +4.4% to $106.42, Brent +5.8% to $114.44 — within striking distance of the $110–$115 threshold analysts identify as a recession signal.

#3
The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) held the federal funds rate at 3.5%–3.75% at its April 28–29 meeting — its third consecutive pause. For the first time, the statement included an easing bias, acknowledging that "developments in the Middle East are contributing to a high level of uncertainty." However, three dissenting governors (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) opposed inclusion of the easing language, viewing inflation risks as more pressing than growth risks. One governor (Miran) wanted an immediate quarter-point cut. Separately, Kevin Warsh — Trump's nominee to replace Jerome Powell as Fed Chair — cleared the Senate Banking Committee on April 29. The full Senate vote is expected May 11, with Warsh taking over on May 15. Warsh has pledged a "regime change": eliminating forward guidance, retiring the dot plot (the quarterly interest-rate forecast chart markets rely on), and refusing to commit to post-meeting press conferences — a significant departure from Powell-era transparency. Warsh claims to support rate cuts on the back of AI-driven disinflation, but his hawkish background (Fed Governor 2006–2011) creates uncertainty about his actual posture. Markets are currently pricing a 94.9% probability of unchanged rates at the June meeting.
