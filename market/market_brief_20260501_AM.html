Market commentary 市场综述

US equities capped their best month since 2020 on April 30, with the S&P 500 rising 1.02% to a record 7,209.01, the Dow surging 790 points to 49,652, and the Russell 2000 — the small-cap index — outperforming with a 2.21% gain, signalling broad-based risk appetite rather than just mega-cap speculation. The rally was driven by a powerful Magnificent Seven earnings wave: Amazon, Microsoft, and Alphabet all reported strong Q1 beats on April 29, overshadowing a post-earnings dip in Meta (which fell on a massive capital-expenditure raise). After the April 30 close, Apple and Eli Lilly both delivered exceptional numbers — Apple reported $111.2B in quarterly revenue (+17%) and guided Q3 growth at +14-17% (double analyst estimates), while Eli Lilly's Mounjaro revenue surged 125% — setting up a positive open for today, May 1. The one note of caution: March PCE inflation came in at 3.5% year-over-year (up from 2.8% in February), partly driven by oil price pressure from the US-Iran war, which has pushed WTI crude above $108 and briefly to $125 intraday — a reminder that the Fed's fight against inflation is not over.

#1
Eli Lilly (LLY) reported Q1 2026 results on April 30 that blew past every estimate: total revenue of $19.8 billion (+56% year-over-year), driven by Mounjaro — its tirzepatide injection for type 2 diabetes — which brought in $8.66 billion worldwide (+125%, far above analyst expectations of $7.26B), and Zepbound — the same molecule approved for obesity — which hit $4.16 billion (+80%). Adjusted EPS came in at $8.55 versus the $7.06 consensus. The company raised its 2026 full-year revenue guidance to $82–85 billion (from $80–83 billion prior) and lifted full-year adjusted EPS to $35.50–$37.00. LLY stock surged 10.54% to close at $901.94 on the day. This caps a remarkable run: Lilly has tripled revenue in three years on the back of the GLP-1 weight-loss drug revolution — a structural, multi-year healthcare shift now worth hundreds of billions of dollars globally. The company is in a two-horse race with Denmark's Novo Nordisk (maker of Ozempic/Wegovy) for dominance in obesity treatment, with demand still vastly exceeding supply.

#3
Meta Platforms reported April 29 after-hours: Q1 2026 revenue of $56.31 billion, up 33% year-over-year and the fastest growth since 2021, comfortably beating the $55.45 billion consensus. Advertising revenue — Meta's core engine — was driven by a 19% increase in ad impressions and 12% higher prices per ad, reflecting AI-powered improvements to ad targeting. AI ad creative tools are now used by 8 million advertisers, and AI-driven ad conversions improved by over 6%. Net income including a large tax benefit was $26.8 billion (EPS $10.44), but stripping out the $8.03 billion Trump tax provision benefit, adjusted EPS was $7.31. The problem: Meta simultaneously raised its 2026 capital expenditure guidance to $125–145 billion (up sharply from prior guidance), citing GPU procurement costs and data centre infrastructure for its AI build-out. This means Meta is pouring cash into infrastructure at a scale that exceeds even Amazon and Google — a bet that AI dominance pays off, but one that compressed near-term free cash flow and sent the stock lower in after-hours trading despite the revenue beat. Meta closed at $669.12 on April 30, well off its 52-week high of $796.25.
