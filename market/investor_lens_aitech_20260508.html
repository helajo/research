#1
"In three years, an agent will consume your software before a human does. Build APIs. Write Markdown for machines. Design for programmatic access... Why are people still telling me what to do? I don't want to do anything. What is the thing I should copy-paste to my agent? Every time I am told 'go to this URL' or 'click here,' I think: no. The industry has to decompose workloads into sensors and actuators over the world."
Via Karpathy's personal blog (Sequoia Ascent 2026 fireside chat summary) · Apr 30, 2026 · View source ↗
Karpathy framed "Software 3.0" — a paradigm where LLMs are the primary interpreter and the context window is the lever. His central claim is that most existing enterprise software is built for humans clicking through screens, not for agents using APIs and tools. He describes MenuGen as a case where "the app shouldn't exist" — multimodal models can now perform the same transformation directly, eliminating the software stack. His test for agent-readiness: can an LLM fully deploy your product end-to-end without manual clicking? Most B2B software fails this test entirely. He predicts every product will need "agent-native surfaces" — MCP servers, structured logs, machine-readable schemas, headless setup — or face displacement by competitors that do.
Market implication 市场含义
CRM NOW TEAM ZM MSFT SNOW
Legacy horizontal SaaS platforms (Salesforce, ServiceNow, Atlassian) are heavily human-UI optimized and face agent-interface risk. Microsoft has the strongest defensive moat given Copilot/Graph API/MCP integration at the OS level — potentially a structural winner. Snowflake's data-API positioning could strengthen. Karpathy's framing is a leading indicator for which categories face disruption from "agent-native" startups (YC W26 is already deploying into these niches — see View #4).
What consensus is missing 共识忽视的关键点
Wall Street models the AI transition as a productivity tailwind for existing software incumbents. Karpathy argues the more important dynamic is structural displacement — "some apps should stop existing as apps" — which implies revenue contraction, not just efficiency gain, for UI-native software.

Karpathy identifies December 2025 as an "agentic inflection point" — when agents crossed from "useful with frequent correction" to "trustworthy for large delegated chunks." His personal workflow shifted from 80% manual coding to delegating nearly everything, with humans focusing on specs, security, taste, and system design judgment. He draws a direct line to hiring: traditional coding interviews (small puzzles) are now measuring the wrong skill. His claim — "people who master agentic workflows may outperform others by far more than 10x" — has direct implications for professional services revenue models built on billable-hour or headcount multipliers.

"The entire 'control and steering' paradigm for AI alignment is fatally flawed. Paraphrased from a16z podcast (Emmett Shear with Erik Torenberg, Nov 2025, recirculating May 2026) · View source ↗
Shear left his role as interim OpenAI CEO and founded Softmax, which has raised from Andreessen Horowitz, Lionheart Ventures, and Plural. Softmax's "organic alignment" theory draws from Michael Levin's lab at Tufts — studying how multi-agent systems develop shared goals, cooperative roles, and collective intelligence through reinforcement learning environments. Shear's critique cuts at the foundations of OpenAI, Anthropic, and DeepMind's alignment programs: if alignment requires genuine goal-sharing rather than behavioral steering, then billions spent on RLHF and Constitutional AI could be inadequate for superintelligence-level systems. The market relevance: if Shear is right, regulatory frameworks built on current alignment paradigms (EU AI Act, NIST standards) may be overhauled — creating policy risk for big AI companies and opportunity for alternative approaches.
