Market commentary 市场综述

Friday capped one of the strongest months in years — the S&P 500 gained approximately 10% in April 2026, its best monthly performance since 2020, and notched a fresh all-time high at 7,230 on the final day of the week. The Nasdaq did even better, adding 0.89% to 25,114, also a record close, as mega-cap technology stocks surged on Apple's blowout earnings. The Dow was the outlier, slipping 0.31% to 49,499, weighed down by energy names after oil fell nearly 2% on hopes of an Iran-US peace agreement. Ten of eleven S&P sectors finished April in positive territory, with technology and communication services the clear leaders; energy and materials lagged on the oil price pullback. Investor sentiment remains broadly risk-on, though the VIX (the market's "fear gauge") crept up slightly to 16.99, signalling that options traders are starting to price in modest uncertainty around this week's flood of tech earnings — AMD, Arm Holdings, and Palantir all report before Friday's critical April non-farm payrolls (jobs) data.

#1
Apple reported its fiscal second quarter (January–March 2026) results after the bell on Thursday, April 30. Revenue of $111.2 billion beat the Street's estimate of $109.66 billion, and diluted earnings per share (EPS — profit per share) of $2.01 beat the consensus of $1.94–$1.95. Services — Apple's software and subscription division covering the App Store, Apple TV+, and Apple Pay — hit a fresh all-time revenue high, growing roughly 16% year over year to an estimated $30.9 billion. The board authorised an additional $100 billion in share buybacks (Apple is literally paying you to own fewer shares at lower prices, which boosts EPS over time) and raised the quarterly dividend by 4%. The one miss: iPhone hardware revenue fell slightly short of analyst estimates for the second time in three quarters, a nuance that points to intensifying competition in China and the broader pressure of Trump-era tariffs on consumer electronics pricing.

Apple guided for June quarter revenue growth of 14–17% year over year — well above the 8–12% range analysts expected. That guidance implies Apple is seeing either strong pre-order demand or a pull-forward effect from customers buying ahead of potential tariff-driven price increases. Apple's stock closed up more than 3% on Friday.

#2
After reporting Q1 2026 earnings late Thursday, Alphabet's (Google's parent company) market capitalisation jumped roughly $240 billion in a single session to approximately $4.65 trillion — putting it within striking distance of Nvidia, which trades around $4.3–4.9 trillion (Nvidia's stock is around $198 with ~24.4 billion shares outstanding). Alphabet's Q1 numbers were exceptional: total revenue +22% to $109.9 billion, net profit surged 81% to $62.6 billion, and — crucially — Google Cloud revenue exploded 63% to $20.03 billion, reflecting an enormous wave of enterprise spending on AI compute. Alphabet's forward P/E (price relative to next year's expected earnings) is 33x, versus Nvidia's leaner 24x — meaning Alphabet is priced more richly on next year's numbers.

The broader significance: for most of the past two years, Nvidia was the poster child of the AI investment boom, as chipmakers benefit first from AI infrastructure buildout. Now Alphabet is challenging that narrative, suggesting markets believe AI application-layer companies (search, cloud, productivity) may capture value as quickly as chip suppliers. Options market odds suggest GOOGL could overtake NVDA as the world's biggest company by market cap as early as mid-May if Nvidia doesn't rally before its own earnings on May 20.

#3
Iran submitted a 14-point counterproposal to the US's nine-point peace framework on Friday, mediated through Pakistan. The proposal's core offer: Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz (the narrow waterway through which roughly 20% of global oil flows — think of it as the world's most important oil pipeline, except it's an ocean passage) if the US lifts its naval blockade on Iranian ports and ceases military operations. The US rejected the proposal swiftly, with Trump stating he could not "imagine it would be acceptable" because it did not address Iran's nuclear programme — which the US demands Iran suspend for at least a decade. The back-and-forth briefly sent WTI crude (US benchmark oil) down nearly 2% to $103.27/barrel on Friday, as investors hoped peace was close, before reality set in that fundamental issues remain unresolved. Oil has surged approximately 60% since the US-Iran war began on February 28, 2026, causing significant downstream inflation pressures across transport, chemicals, and consumer goods. The Fed has explicitly flagged Middle East developments as an "uncertainty" adding risk to its inflation outlook.
