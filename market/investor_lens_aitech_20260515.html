Andrej Karpathy 17.5/20 MSFT AMZN CRM ↓ NOW ↓ vs SaaS consensus Published Apr 30, 2026 · Active this week
"Some apps should stop existing as apps" — Software 3.0 makes entire categories of traditional software spurious, not just slower
"某些应用程序根本不应该继续存在" —— Software 3.0时代，AI将直接取代传统软件整个类别，而非仅仅提速
#1
"AI is not just a faster way to build the old apps. Some apps should stop existing as apps... The neural network directly transforms input media into output media. All of MenuGen is spurious in that framing. It is working in the old paradigm. That app shouldn't exist. In the Software 3.0 paradigm, the neural network does more of the work."

"Traditional computers automate what you can specify in code. LLMs and reinforcement learning automate what you can verify."

"You can outsource your thinking, but you can't outsource your understanding."
Via Karpathy blog (verbatim) · Sequoia Ascent 2026 fireside chat summary, published Apr 30, 2026 · View source ↗ · Also posted to X: @karpathy ↗
Karpathy's Sequoia Ascent 2026 talk — published as a long-form blog post and circulating widely this week — frames December 2025 as a genuine agentic inflection point where "the chunks just came out fine" and the unit of programming shifted from typing lines of code to delegating macro-actions to agents. More importantly for markets, he distinguishes "vibe coding" (which raises the floor for all builders) from "agentic engineering" (which raises the ceiling far beyond the old "10x engineer" concept). The market-relevant claim is not that AI helps coders — that's already priced. The claim is that when a neural network can directly transform input to output, entire applications cease to exist as businesses. His MenuGen example is illustrative but the extrapolation is existential for the SaaS application layer. He also offers a founder playbook: find domains that are economically valuable and structurally verifiable but not yet targeted by frontier lab RL training runs — these are the startup wedges Wall Street hasn't mapped.
Market implication · 市场含义
MSFT AMZN GOOGL CRM ↓ NOW ↓ WDAY ↓ ZEN ↓
Bearish on application-layer SaaS companies (CRM, NOW, WDAY, ZEN) that sell point solutions now replicable by a multimodal model call. Bullish on agent-native infrastructure providers — cloud hyperscalers (MSFT Azure MCP server ecosystem, AMZN AWS) and inference plays — whose platforms become the host process rather than scaffolding around it. The "agent-native infrastructure" framing in the YC RFS this week independently reinforces the same thesis (see View #3).
What consensus is missing · 共识忽视的关键点
Wall Street's AI trade is still "AI accelerates existing software companies." Karpathy's thesis — rooted in direct model capability observation — is that AI eliminates entire software categories rather than accelerating them. Sell-side SaaS models do not model this probability at all.

#3
Paraphrased from YC Summer 2026 RFS (April 28, 2026) and Garry Tan public statements: "The moat that protected legacy SaaS — millions of lines of code built over decades — is gone. AI has collapsed the cost of producing software by 10 to 100x. A five-person startup can now outbuild entrenched incumbents on specific workflows." YC explicitly names ERP, chip design software, industrial control systems, and supply chain management as target categories. On the business model shift: "AI-native companies don't sell software — they sell the service. Instead of giving you a tool, they just do the work." On AI strategy theater: "In 2026, AI strategy that is not tied to a specific workflow, KPI, and operating model is mostly theater."
Paraphrased from YC Summer 2026 Requests for Startups · View YC RFS ↗ · Analysis: VC Cafe, Apr 28, 2026 ↗ · Deep dive: Epsilla, May 2, 2026 ↗
The YC Summer 2026 RFS is not just a recruitment document — it is the best publicly available signal of where the smartest early-stage founders are going. Three elements stand out for market implications. First, YC explicitly calls for "SaaS Challengers" targeting ERP, supply chain, and industrial control systems — categories that were considered defensible by scale and switching costs. Second, it introduces "Software for Agents" as a priority category, envisioning the next trillion users as AI agents and calling for machine-first APIs, CLIs, and MCPs to replace GUI-centric software. Third, it signals a business model inversion: AI-native companies should sell outcomes (a completed tax filing, an audited report) rather than software seats — a model that competes with services outsourcing rather than existing SaaS vendors. The February 2026 "SaaSpocalypse" already wiped ~$285 billion from SaaS valuations. YC's RFS suggests the structural pressure has not reversed.

SAP ↓ CRM ↓ NOW ↓ WDAY ↓ MSFT AMZN
Directional bearish signal on legacy enterprise SaaS (SAP, CRM, NOW, WDAY) where switching costs were historically the primary moat — YC is now explicitly telling founders to attack these categories. Bullish on hyperscalers as the new infrastructure layer for agent-native applications. Note: this is a multi-year structural view, not a near-term catalyst; use as a lens for multiple expansion/compression rather than near-term earnings guidance.
What consensus is missing · 共识忽视的关键点
Sell-side SaaS upgrades post-SaaSpocalypse are framing the February selloff as an overreaction and calling for recovery. YC's RFS — which reflects where the best founders are actively building — suggests the competitive threat is accelerating, not plateauing. The gap between sell-side recovery thesis and founder-level competitive reality is widening.

Delian Asparouhov · Founders Fund 14.5/20 AI apps ↓ HADR-pvt negative GM blind spot TBPN podcast, May 2026
The AI app layer is full of companies "selling a buck for 90 cents" — investors are not distinguishing temporary negative gross margins from permanently uneconomic ones
AI应用层充斥着"一美元换九毛钱"的公司——投资者未能区分暂时性负毛利率与永久性不经济模式
#4
Paraphrased from TBPN podcast (May 2026) and associated LinkedIn/X posts: Asparouhov argued that "today money's piling into AI apps and models where some unit economics look like 'selling a buck for 90 cents.'" He contrasted this with hard-tech companies like Hadrian (precision manufacturing) which were "deeply negative gross margin to start, but now have super limited S&M because there are only 10–15 customers that matter and once you deliver, they proactively throw revenue at you." The implicit warning: most AI app companies will not follow the Hadrian path because they lack the physical-world moat that collapses customer acquisition costs at scale.
Paraphrased from TBPN podcast and X post, May 2026 · @tbpn X post ↗ · LinkedIn clip: View clip ↗
Asparouhov's argument is a targeted attack on a specific blind spot in the current AI investment cycle. The market is pricing many AI application companies on revenue growth, implicitly accepting negative gross margins as temporary infrastructure costs on the path to defensibility. Asparouhov's contrarian view — grounded in Founders Fund's preference for hard-tech companies with physical-world moats — is that most AI app companies will never escape the inference cost treadmill. The companies that do earn negative margins while building durable moats are exceptions, not the rule, and they share a specific structural feature: a small, captive customer set where marginal cost of serving the next unit approaches zero. AI horizontal SaaS companies with broad, price-sensitive SMB customers do not have this structure.
Market implication · 市场含义
AI app layer ↓ NVDA AMZN
Bearish on AI application companies that are scaling revenue on negative or near-zero gross margins and targeting horizontal markets — these will not follow the Uber/DoorDash defensibility path. Indirectly bullish on NVDA and inference infrastructure (the companies selling the "shovels" to companies effectively burning capital on compute). Note: most negative-GM AI app companies are private; the public market implication is screening public AI SaaS names for gross margin trajectory before trusting revenue growth multiples.
What consensus is missing · 共识忽视的关键点
Current AI app valuations — both public and private — embed the assumption that inference costs will compress fast enough to convert negative GMs into software-like margins. Asparouhov's counter is that defensibility, not just cost compression, determines whether this works; and most horizontal AI apps have no path to the captive-customer-set moat that makes negative GMs temporary.

Emmett Shear · Softmax 13.0/20 GOOGL MSFT vs control-paradigm safety a16z podcast, May 2026
The entire AI safety "control and steering" paradigm is fatally flawed — enterprise AI deployments built on it face a competitive moat problem as "organic alignment" approaches emerge
AI安全领域的"控制与引导"范式存在根本性缺陷——基于此范式构建的企业AI部署将面临护城河竞争挑战
#5
Paraphrased from a16z podcast with Erik Torenberg (May 2026): Shear argues that the dominant AI safety paradigm — focused on control, instruction-following, and steering — is fundamentally the wrong approach. His alternative, "organic alignment," draws an analogy to how cells cooperate to form a body, and how humans cooperate to form societies. True alignment, he argues, is a capability (the ability to genuinely understand and care about human norms) rather than a constraint (rules imposed externally). AI systems built on control paradigms are fragile and misuse-prone in ways that organically aligned systems would not be. He also argues treating AGI as a tool rather than a potential being "could be catastrophic."
Paraphrased from a16z podcast, May 2026 · View source ↗ · Also covered at: Cognitive Revolution ↗
Shear co-founded Softmax in March 2025 specifically to pursue organic alignment research, having concluded during his brief OpenAI tenure that the field was heading in the wrong direction. His public market relevance is indirect but real: large enterprise deployments of AI are increasingly differentiated by safety and alignment approach. If Shear's organic alignment thesis gains traction — and the a16z platform is a significant distribution mechanism — it could shift enterprise buying criteria in ways that disadvantage companies (including Google DeepMind and Anthropic products) built around rules-based RLHF alignment. The implication is most speculative of this week's views, but the source credibility is high and the podcast distribution broad.
Market implication · 市场含义
GOOGL MSFT
Most directly relevant to GOOGL (DeepMind's control-paradigm safety work) and MSFT (Anthropic partnership). If enterprise buyers begin evaluating AI systems on "genuine alignment" vs. "rule-following," it introduces a new axis of competitive risk that current sell-side models do not price. This is a 2–3 year risk factor, not a near-term catalyst. Public company implication is indirect since alignment approaches are primarily a differentiator for private frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Softmax itself).
What consensus is missing · 共识忽视的关键点
Markets treat AI safety as a regulatory/compliance issue — a cost center. Shear's framing treats it as a capability and competitive moat. If he is right, alignment approach becomes a first-order product differentiator, creating winners and losers among frontier labs that are not currently priced into parent company valuations.
