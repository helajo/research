3. Business overview

Bloom Energy (San Jose, CA; incorporated 2001) designs, manufactures, sells and installs solid oxide fuel cell (SOFC) systems for on-site power generation. Its core product, the Bloom Energy Server, converts natural gas, biogas, hydrogen, or blends into electricity through a non-combustion electrochemical process — deployable in months rather than the multi-year wait for grid interconnections or gas turbines. It also sells the Bloom Electrolyzer for hydrogen production.

Customers span utilities, data centers (the key growth driver — partnerships with American Electric Power, Equinix, Oracle, and the Brookfield AI-infrastructure framework), retail, healthcare, education, telecom and manufacturing, sold through direct and indirect channels in the US and internationally (South Korea via SK ecoplant is a long-standing market). Revenue splits across product, installation, service, and electricity (per the FY2025 10-K filed 2026-02-09).

中文摘要：Bloom Energy 设计和制造固体氧化物燃料电池（SOFC）系统，用于现场发电，核心产品 Bloom Energy Server 可将天然气、沼气或氢气通过非燃烧的电化学过程转化为电力，部署周期仅需数月。数据中心是关键增长引擎，合作伙伴包括 AEP、Equinix、Oracle 和 Brookfield。收入分为产品、安装、服务和电力四个部分。

7. Industry analysis

The AI power bottleneck is the story of 2025–2026. Data-center demand has overwhelmed the US grid: interconnection queues stretch years, and the scramble for natural-gas plants has created a gas-turbine shortage — per TechCrunch (Apr 2026), turbine prices are expected to end 2026 up ~195% vs 2019, with wait times of five to seven years for large units. That pushes hyperscalers toward anything deployable now.

Fuel cells are a prime beneficiary. Rystad Energy projects 10.4 GW of cumulative data-center fuel-cell demand 2026–2030 (investment reaching ~$30B by 2030) and models ~40% of projected 2030 US data-center capacity pursuing dedicated on-site power. Solid oxide fuel cells — Bloom's technology — hold roughly 48% of the data-center fuel-cell market (Persistence Market Research), and Goldman Sachs has highlighted fuel cells' months-not-years deployment and 10–30% better fuel efficiency vs turbines.

Bloom's position: the at-scale leader. It is the only SOFC vendor with GW-scale manufacturing, ~30% gross margins, hyperscaler references (Oracle, Equinix), utility channels (AEP), and now a $25B capital partner (Brookfield). FCEL is a distant, unprofitable competitor; the real long-term competition is gas turbines (GEV, Caterpillar, Cummins gensets) once supply normalizes, plus nuclear SMRs post-2030.

Headwinds: elevated natural-gas prices (Middle East conflict) worsen fuel-cell operating economics; turbine supply eventually catches up; policy support for clean energy has become less generous under current US policy.

中文摘要：AI 数据中心电力短缺是行业核心逻辑：电网接入排队数年，燃气轮机价格较 2019 年上涨约 195%、交付周期 5–7 年，迫使超大规模数据中心转向可快速部署的现场电源。Rystad 预测 2026–2030 年数据中心燃料电池累计需求 10.4 GW，2030 年投资规模达 $300 亿。Bloom 是 SOFC 领域唯一规模化且盈利的龙头（该技术占数据中心燃料电池市场约 48%）。逆风：天然气价格高企、轮机供应终将恢复、清洁能源政策支持减弱。

12. Macro sensitivity

  • Interest rates — high exposure, partly hedged. Fed on hold at 3.50–3.75% (June 2026 FOMC), with some officials leaning toward hikes as PCE inflation runs ~3.6%. Long-duration growth stocks like BE (value in far-future earnings) are rate-sensitive, and customer projects are financing-heavy — though the Brookfield capital partnership blunts the project-financing side.
  • Natural-gas prices — direct negative. The Middle East conflict has kept energy costs elevated (EIA: not normalizing until ~2028). Bloom's servers consume gas; expensive fuel worsens the levelized cost vs grid power. Partly offset because the alternative (turbines) burns more gas per kWh and can't be delivered anyway.
  • Tariffs/trade — moderate. Tariffs are feeding the ~3.6% inflation; Bloom manufactures in the US (a relative advantage) but imports components; tariff-driven capex inflation raises system costs.
  • Enterprise/AI capex cycle — the whole thesis. BE is effectively a leveraged bet on hyperscaler data-center capex continuing at record pace. Any AI-capex digestion phase hits BE harder than the hyperscalers themselves (beta 3.74).
  • Net read: today's macro is a tailwind on the demand side (grid scarcity, AI capex) and a headwind on rates and fuel costs. The demand tailwind currently dominates — that's what the +457% says.

17. Risks

  1. Valuation/expectation risk (dominant): at 34× sales, even strong results can disappoint a market pricing perfection; the base-case scenario grid implies downside.
  2. Framework-conversion risk: the $25B Brookfield figure is a financing envelope; slow conversion to firm MW orders would deflate the story.
  3. Fuel-cost & competition risk: sustained high natural-gas prices erode customer economics; when gas-turbine supply normalizes (or SMRs arrive ~2030+), Bloom's scarcity premium fades.
  4. Customer concentration: historically reliant on large single relationships (SK ecoplant in Korea; now Brookfield/AEP/Oracle) — a stumble at one partner matters (10-K risk factor).
  5. Dilution & leverage: converts + SBC keep growing the share count; 12% short interest amplifies volatility in both directions.
