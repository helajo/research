SECTION 1 · 第一节
Company Background & Business Overview
公司背景与业务概述

KLA Corporation — formerly KLA-Tencor Corporation, rebranded in July 2019 — was incorporated in 1975 and is headquartered in Milpitas, California. Over five decades it has evolved from a niche wafer inspection startup into the undisputed global leader in semiconductor process control, yield management, and metrology. With approximately 15,000 employees worldwide, KLA's technologies sit at the intersection of every major semiconductor node transition, making it indispensable to chip manufacturers ranging from TSMC and Samsung to Intel and NAND players like SK Hynix and Micron.

KLA operates through four reportable segments: Semiconductor Process Control (the dominant engine, contributing ~75% of revenue via wafer inspection, patterning, metrology and review systems); Specialty Semiconductor Process (etch, deposition, and plasma dicing for specialty fabs); PCB, Display and Component Inspection (imaging and electrical testing for the PCB and display markets); and Other (OEM components and refurbished equipment). The services business — spanning recurring revenue from installed-base contracts — contributes roughly 23% of revenue and grows at a more predictable mid-teens pace, providing a structural floor through downturns.

The business model is durable and high-moat: as chips become more complex at advanced nodes (3nm, 2nm, and below), defects become exponentially more costly. Every additional layer of patterning or 3D integration requires more — and more sophisticated — process control. KLA's tools are the feedback loop. Its addressable market in wafer fab equipment is expected to exceed $140 billion in 2026, and KLA's share of the process control sub-segment is estimated at 50–55% globally, a near-monopoly position built over decades of R&D investment and customer lock-in through proprietary data analytics software (Cimetric, GroupSystems).

The company has a remarkable capital return track record, having just announced its 17th consecutive annual dividend increase (to $2.30/share/quarter, a 21% increase). A 10-for-1 forward stock split effective June 12, 2026 will broaden retail accessibility without affecting fundamentals. CEO Rick Wallace has led KLA since 2006, providing exceptional continuity and execution discipline.

SECTION 6 · 第六节
Growth Catalysts & Competitive Moat
增长催化剂与竞争护城河

1. AI Infrastructure Buildout: The insatiable demand for AI accelerators — NVIDIA H100/B200, AMD MI300, custom ASICs at TSMC N3/N2 — requires the most precise process control in semiconductor history. At 3nm and below, any defect in a gate-all-around (GAA) transistor or buried power rail is catastrophic. KLA's tools are the irreplaceable quality filter in this process. Management has directly linked its Q3 FY2026 momentum to AI infrastructure buildout across foundry/logic, memory (HBM), and advanced packaging.

2. Advanced Packaging Surge: Advanced packaging (CoWoS, HBM stacking, chiplets) is the fastest-growing vector for KLA. Revenue from this segment is forecast to exceed $925M in calendar 2025 — up 85% year-over-year — versus $500M in 2024. KLA's inspection and metrology tools are essential to verify heterogeneous integration where the inter-die connections are nanometre-scale. This is a multi-year structural growth driver disconnected from traditional fab cycle timing.

3. E-Beam Inspection Expansion: KLA has doubled its e-beam inspection revenues, as leading-edge fabs shift from optical to electron-beam inspection for the most critical layers. E-beam systems command higher ASP and carry superior margins. This shift is driven by the physics of defect detection at sub-3nm geometries — optical wavelengths simply cannot resolve them.

4. WFE Market Expansion: KLA forecasts the overall wafer fab equipment (WFE) market to exceed $140B in 2026. The company's 2030 target model (shared at March 2026 Investor Day) projects $14.8B revenue and $5.3B earnings, implying ~22% revenue growth from FY2025 levels. Management reiterated "durable value creation" confidence via the new $7B buyback and 17th consecutive dividend increase.

5. Competitive Moat: KLA's moat has four reinforcing layers: (a) technology lock-in — switching costs are enormous as fabs build process recipes around KLA data; (b) installed base — 23% services revenue is near-contractual and grows as the base expands; (c) data network effects — KLA's analytics software (GroupSystems) aggregates defect data across customers, improving its models and making the software stickier over time; (d) R&D scale — at ~$2B+ annual R&D spend, KLA maintains technology leads that smaller players cannot match.

🏭 Fab Diversification (CHIPS Act)
CHIPS Act subsidies are driving new fab builds in the US (TSMC Arizona, Intel Ohio, Samsung Texas). Each new fab requires a complete KLA tool set from day one. Japan (Rapidus), Germany (Intel Magdeburg), and India also present greenfield opportunities.
晶圆厂多元化（芯片法案）：美国《芯片法案》推动台积电（亚利桑那州）、英特尔、三星等新建晶圆厂，每座新厂均需配置全套KLA工具。日本、德国、印度的新建项目亦提供增量机遇。
⚠️ Export Controls Headwind
US BIS export controls have reduced KLA's China revenue share from 41–44% (FY2024) to ~30% (FY2025), with guidance for further moderation to high-20s in FY2026. Jefferies estimates a $300–350M revenue impact in CY2026. Management is actively diversifying to Korea (15%) and Taiwan (27%).

The semiconductor equipment sector has entered a new phase: the AI-driven "process control intensity" cycle, where chips that are harder to build require more KLA tools per unit of output. Unlike prior cycles driven by unit volume, this cycle is driven by chip complexity. Even a flat or declining unit volume environment would support KLA's business if node transitions continue. The primary risk is a demand pause — if hyperscalers defer CAPEX, orders could slow. However, backlog visibility from the $8.9B RPO and the services base provides a cushion.

SECTION 8 · 第八节
Geopolitical, Macro & Regulatory Risk
地缘政治、宏观与监管风险
🚨 China Export Controls
The most material risk. US BIS regulations have progressively restricted advanced wafer fab equipment sales to China. KLA's China revenue fell from ~41% (FY2024) to ~30% (FY2025). Further rules targeting mature-node equipment could extend the headwind beyond advanced fabs. Management estimates a ~100bp gross margin headwind from tariffs. Jefferies forecasts a $300–350M CY2026 revenue impact. Active lobbying and licence applications provide some mitigation.
⚠️ Semiconductor Cycle Risk
KLA's revenue is tied to semiconductor CAPEX cycles, which are inherently volatile. A prolonged NAND over-supply (as seen in 2023–24) or memory CAPEX pullback would reduce order flows. However, KLA's FY2025 results demonstrated resilience: even with China headwinds, revenue grew 21% as leading-edge logic and advanced packaging demand filled the gap.
⚠️ Tariff Margin Headwind
Service-related tariffs on components imported and serviced internationally create a ~100 basis point gross margin headwind per quarter. Management is actively using global manufacturing footprint flexibility and logistics optimisation to mitigate, but this remains a cost pressure for as long as tariff regimes persist.
ℹ️ Valuation / Macro Risk
At ~39x NTM P/E — well above the 5-year average of ~27x — the stock carries significant multiple compression risk if AI CAPEX sentiment turns. Rising interest rates or a macro slowdown compressing semiconductor demand could trigger a meaningful de-rating. CEO insider selling in May 2026 (4,512 shares) adds a cautionary note, though this may be pre-scheduled.
Geopolitical Summary: KLA has navigated the China export control shift with relative resilience — redirecting revenue to Korea (+growth) and Taiwan (+growth). However, approximately 25–30% of revenue still comes from China (FY2026 guidance), and any escalatory measures could create a near-term earnings shock. The scenario where U.S. controls expand to "legacy node" equipment (~28nm and above, which China fabs buy in quantity) would be the most damaging surprise.

What This Layer Does · 该层的作用

Layer 1 is the Silicon Brain — the foundation of the entire AI infrastructure stack. It encompasses the design, manufacturing, and quality-assurance of the physical chips that power all AI computation. Semiconductor equipment companies like KLA are the enabling infrastructure for chip manufacturing: without process control and yield management, no advanced chip can be produced reliably at scale.

Why KLAC Belongs Here · 为何归属此层

KLA Corporation is the dominant process control and yield management equipment vendor for semiconductor fabs globally. Its wafer inspection, patterning metrology, and review systems are deployed at every leading-edge fab (TSMC, Samsung, Intel, SK Hynix) to detect nanometre-scale defects before chips fail. KLA does not make chips — it makes the quality assurance infrastructure that makes chips possible, placing it squarely in Layer 1 alongside chip designers and EDA tools. The AI boom requires ever-more-complex chips at advanced nodes (3nm, 2nm), driving exponentially more demand for KLA's process control tools per wafer start.
