SECTION 1 · 第一节
Company Background — 60 Years of Photonics, Three Eras of Reinvention
公司背景 — 60年光子学历史，三次战略蜕变
The company trading under the COHR ticker today is the product of a complex, decade-long consolidation of the global photonics industry. To understand it, you need to understand three distinct eras.
Era 1 — The Original Coherent (1966–2021): Founded in 1966 in Santa Clara, California, Coherent Inc. was one of the pioneering laser companies of the Silicon Valley era. For 55 years it built precision lasers for scientific research, semiconductor manufacturing (laser annealers, excimer lasers for LASIK surgery), and industrial processing. It was a respected but relatively modest photonics business — profitable, niche, growing slowly.
Era 2 — II-VI's Transformation (2021–2022): The pivotal moment came when II-VI Incorporated — a Pennsylvania-based manufacturer of compound semiconductors, optical components, and laser technology — acquired Finisar Corporation in 2019 for $3.2 billion. Finisar was the world's largest manufacturer of optical transceivers, the modules used in datacentre and telecom networks to transmit data as light. This made II-VI a major datacom player overnight. Then in January 2022, II-VI completed a $6.8 billion acquisition of the original Coherent Inc., creating a photonics giant spanning lasers, optical components, transceivers, compound semiconductors (InP, GaAs, SiC), and industrial systems. The combined company rebranded as Coherent Corp in June 2022 and trades on the NYSE under the original COHR ticker.
Era 3 — AI Photonics Platform (2023–present): The newly combined Coherent spent 2022–2024 digesting the acquisitions, paying down debt, and restructuring. During this period the stock languished while the market processed the merger complexity. Then in late 2024, as AI infrastructure demand exploded, Coherent's datacentre business — driven by demand for 400G, 800G, and 1.6T optical transceivers and InP laser chips — became the growth engine of the entire company. The stock began a dramatic re-rating. In March 2026, NVIDIA cemented Coherent's strategic position with a $2 billion equity investment and a multi-billion-dollar, multi-year purchase commitment for optical products. Today Coherent is unambiguously the most comprehensive, vertically integrated photonics platform in the world, led by CEO Jim Anderson (formerly CEO of GlobalFoundries) since 2024.
Coherent employs FMP approximately 26,157 people globally, operates manufacturing facilities across the US, Europe, and Asia, and sells to every major hyperscaler, networking OEM, industrial equipment maker, and research institution worldwide. Its fiscal year ends June 30.

The financial trajectory of Coherent is one of the most dramatic V-shaped recoveries in the technology sector. After the II-VI/Coherent merger closed in 2022, the combined company carried $4B+ in long-term debt, was integrating three major acquisitions simultaneously, and was navigating a telecom capex downcycle. From FY2022 to FY2024, the stock fell more than 60% from its peak as the market processed this complexity.
Then AI changed everything. The demand for high-speed optical transceivers exploded as hyperscalers built out GPU clusters. Coherent's datacentre and communications segment revenue surged, margins expanded, and debt was systematically paid down. FY2025 non-GAAP EPS of $3.53 represented 191% growth year-on-year — a figure that barely anyone outside specialist photonics investors was tracking. By the time the broader market noticed in late 2024 and 2025, the stock had already moved significantly. The Q3 FY2026 results — $1.81B revenue, 39.6% non-GAAP gross margin, $1.41 non-GAAP EPS — confirm the acceleration is real and continuing.

SECTION 6 · 第六节
The NVIDIA Partnership — A Strategic Game-Changer
英伟达战略合作 — 改变游戏规则的重大事件
The March 2, 2026 announcement of NVIDIA's $2 billion strategic investment in Coherent is the single most important event in the company's recent history. It deserves its own analysis because it is more than a financial transaction — it is a statement about which company NVIDIA believes will be the foundation of the next decade of AI optical infrastructure.
What the deal actually includes:
  • $2 billion equity investment — NVIDIA purchased 7,788,161 shares at $256.80 per share (a modest premium to the then-market price). NVIDIA now holds approximately 4% of Coherent's enlarged share count, making it a meaningful strategic shareholder with board-level alignment incentives
  • Multi-billion-dollar purchase commitment — NVIDIA committed to a multi-year, multi-billion-dollar purchase of Coherent's advanced laser and optical networking products. The exact value is not disclosed, but at NVIDIA's current scale of AI factory deployment, even a modest allocation is transformative for Coherent's revenue
  • Access to five additional product families for CPO — NVIDIA granted Coherent development access and co-investment rights across five co-packaged optics product families. This is the most strategically important element: CPO integrates optics directly onto NVIDIA's switch ASICs, and Coherent is the partner being brought inside the chip architecture
  • Silicon photonics co-development — Joint development of silicon photonics technology for next-generation AI switch architecture. NVIDIA stated: "With Coherent, NVIDIA is pioneering next-generation silicon photonics to enable AI infrastructure at unprecedented scale, speed and energy efficiency"
  • US manufacturing expansion — The investment specifically supports Coherent's US-based manufacturing buildout — strategically aligned with both NVIDIA's supply chain diversification goals and US government reshoring objectives
  • 5 GW AI factory co-investment — Coherent and NVIDIA are working together on a 5 gigawatt AI factory buildout by 2030 — an extraordinary scale commitment that validates the multi-year nature of the optical supply demand
Why this matters structurally: NVIDIA does not make equity investments casually. The last comparable photonics investment was NVIDIA's parallel $2B investment in Lumentum on the same day — totalling $4B in a single day to secure optical supply. This signals that NVIDIA views photonics as the binding constraint on AI infrastructure scaling — not GPUs, not memory, not power — optical interconnects. By investing in and locking up preferred supply from both major InP laser suppliers, NVIDIA is essentially pre-paying for the optical capacity it knows it will need for the GB300, Vera Rubin, and beyond product generations. For Coherent, this converts a portion of its future revenue into effectively pre-committed strategic demand at the highest-quality customer relationship possible.

SECTION 7 · 第七节
Growth Catalysts & Competitive Moat
增长催化剂与竞争护城河
Catalyst 1 — AI Transceiver Demand Super-Cycle: Every AI GPU cluster requires optical transceivers for intra-cluster, inter-rack, and inter-datacenter connectivity. As NVIDIA ships increasingly dense GPU clusters (GB200 NVL72 at 1.6Tbps per switch port), the transceiver speed requirement doubles roughly every 18 months (400G → 800G → 1.6T → 3.2T → 6.4T). Coherent is on this roadmap at every step. The company estimates the datacenter optical SAM will grow to $44B by 2030 — a roughly 4x expansion from today.
Catalyst 2 — Co-Packaged Optics (CPO) Revolution: The transition from pluggable transceivers to CPO — where optics are integrated directly onto the switch package — is the next multi-year wave. CPO eliminates electrical cable losses, reduces power consumption by 30–40%, and enables much higher bandwidth density. Coherent's NVIDIA partnership specifically targets CPO development, and the company demonstrated a 6.4T socketed SiPho CPO system at OFC 2026. CPO revenue is expected to ramp meaningfully in 2027–2028 and could add a new revenue stream at higher margins than pluggable transceivers.
Catalyst 3 — InP Scarcity Moat: InP wafer supply is structurally constrained by the difficulty of growing high-quality InP crystals. Coherent manufactures its own InP wafers through its Finisar heritage and Compound Semiconductor operations. With InP capacity sold out through 2027 and book-to-bill above 4x in the DC&C segment, Coherent is in a seller's market. The NVIDIA investment partially functions as capacity reservation — securing access to future InP supply.
Catalyst 4 — Industrial Laser Recovery: The industrial segment (25% of revenue) has been flat-to-declining as factory automation capex was deferred through the 2023–2024 macro uncertainty. As industrial production recovers globally, laser demand for precision manufacturing, EV battery welding, and clean energy processing should recover. Management guided for improving Industrial demand in H2 FY2026 and through FY2027 — this segment recovery would be an incremental earnings driver above the AI narrative.
Catalyst 5 — SiC Substrate Growth: Coherent's SiC substrate business (within Industrial) is quietly becoming strategically important as EV manufacturers and power electronics producers scale production. SiC enables more efficient power conversion in EV drivetrains. Coherent competes with Wolfspeed in 150mm and 200mm SiC wafers. As the EV industry recovers from its 2024–2025 slowdown, SiC substrate demand should improve.
The moat — vertical integration depth: The competitive moat is Coherent's vertical integration — it is one of the only companies that can supply customers from raw semiconductor crystal to finished optical system. This creates four layers of protection: (1) material scarcity (InP crystal growth is expertise-intensive and cannot be rapidly expanded); (2) manufacturing complexity (wafer fab, chip packaging, module assembly — each a specialised capability); (3) customer qualification cycles (once a Coherent transceiver is qualified in an NVIDIA switch or hyperscaler system, switching supplier takes 12–24 months); (4) software and systems co-development (the CPO and OCS products require deep co-engineering with NVIDIA and switch OEMs — creating relationship moats beyond product specs).

SECTION 8 · 第八节
Key Risks
主要风险
📉 Valuation Premium Risk
At $382, the stock is 49% above NVIDIA's $256.80 investment price. The trailing PE of 158x and forward PE of ~51x are elevated. Pre-Q3 earnings analyst median target of ~$250–$320 implies the stock has run significantly ahead of consensus. Any guidance miss or macro risk-off event could trigger a severe multiple compression.
📦 Goodwill Impairment
~$7.6B in goodwill and intangible assets from the II-VI/Finisar/Coherent mergers remains on the balance sheet. If the datacenter business materially disappoints sustained expectations, accounting tests could trigger goodwill write-downs that would devastate GAAP equity — though this would not affect the operating business or non-GAAP earnings.
🔬 Technology Transition Risk
The transition from pluggable transceivers to CPO could cannibalize Coherent's existing transceiver revenue stream before CPO revenue fully ramps. If the transition happens faster than expected, there could be a 2–3 quarter revenue gap. If it happens slower, competitors may close the technology gap.
⚡ High Beta — Extreme Volatility
Beta of 2.053 means COHR moves ~2x the broader market. The 52-week range of $73 to $413 captures this — a nearly 5x range in 12 months. Investors must be comfortable with 20–30% drawdowns on macro risk-off days while holding a fundamentally sound thesis.
🏭 Manufacturing Execution
Scaling InP wafer production, silicon photonics capacity, and US-based manufacturing simultaneously is operationally complex. Any yield issues, equipment delays, or cleanroom incidents could miss quarterly guidance — which at current valuations would be severely punished. The company has already guided Q4 FY26 revenue at a wide range of $1.91B–$2.05B, partly reflecting manufacturing ramp uncertainty.
🔄 Share Count Dilution
Shares outstanding increased 16.99% in one year — from ~155M to ~196M — primarily due to the NVIDIA placement and merger-related equity instruments. Ongoing stock-based compensation (~$150M per quarter) continues to dilute existing shareholders. EPS growth must meaningfully outpace share count growth for per-share value creation to materialise.
🌏 China Exposure
Coherent has manufacturing operations in China and sells into Chinese datacom and telecom markets. US-China export control escalation — particularly around compound semiconductors like InP and GaAs — could create supply chain disruptions or market access restrictions. The industrial laser business also has significant China revenue exposure.
⚔️ Competitive Pressure
Lumentum is aggressively expanding EML capacity (NVIDIA invested $2B in both). MACOM, Marvell (SiPho ASICs), and Broadcom all have optical ambitions. Vertically integrated Chinese competitors (HiSilicon, Accelink) may offer lower-cost alternatives in non-restricted markets. Coherent's moat is deep but not impenetrable.
