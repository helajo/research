NVDA: The Aug 26 Print, Setup Through Reaction

A clean beat on every line and a t0 reaction that extended instead of fading — then gave back more than half of that gain the very next session, mostly on a broad chip-sector selloff rather than a reversal of sentiment on the print itself. By the next trading day after that, the stock had steadied comfortably above both the pre-print close and its prior peak.

NVDA walked into this print already down, not up. The stock ran from a recent peak of $225.30 on Aug 13 to a low of $208.48 on Aug 24, then drifted to $209.66 at the Aug 26 close (the last print before the report) — still 6.2% off the peak. That pullback was layered on top of a broader chip-sector selloff (SOXX fell roughly 5% on Aug 18 and another 2.7% on Aug 24). There was no "priced-for-perfection" run-up to unwind here, and no cushion of recent gains either.

Consensus sat at $2.09 EPS. NVDA had beaten every quarter for years, and over the four quarters immediately before this one the beat had settled into a tight 3.5–5.5% range — well down from the double-digit beats of the 2023–24 AI-boom peak. With a beat rate that close to automatic, the real question wasn't "will it beat," it was whether the beat stayed in that recent range (confirmation, not news) and what guidance said about the quarter after this one.

The base rate this print was tested against: NVDA's prior print (May 20, 2026) beat cleanly by 5.54% — squarely inside the usual band — and the stock still closed down 1.90% that day, then kept sliding to -8.11% by ten trading days out, even though the sector itself was roughly flat-to-up that same session. A clean beat had not reliably meant a positive reaction recently. As the next section shows, this print broke that pattern.

This is precisely the test the base-rate caution above was set up to check: last time, a clean beat inside the usual band still produced a losing two weeks because guidance and margin tone disappointed. This time the beat came in above that recent range and guidance beat too. Both of the ingredients that were missing in May were present this time.

Quick note on GAAP vs. non-GAAP: GAAP diluted EPS was actually higher this quarter, at $2.46, versus the $2.22 non-GAAP figure everyone is quoting (and the one consensus is measured against). The gap is almost entirely a $7.77B unrealized gain on NVIDIA's equity investment holdings that flows into GAAP net income but gets excluded from non-GAAP as non-operating. That's the less common direction for a GAAP/non-GAAP gap to run — usually a one-time charge depresses GAAP below adjusted; here a one-time investment gain inflates GAAP above it. The $2.22 non-GAAP number is the more honest read of the actual chip business, and it's also the more conservative of the two this quarter.

Behind the Numbers: What Management Said

Four things from the earnings call itself explain why the beat and the guidance were so strong — each one in plain terms.

1. NVIDIA can't build chips fast enough — it's not a demand problem

Most companies worry about whether customers will buy enough of what they make. NVIDIA's problem right now is the opposite: management said its supply chain can support roughly 70% growth, but customer demand is higher than that. In plain terms: NVIDIA is sold out. The $108B guidance for next quarter isn't "as much as customers want" — it's "as much as NVIDIA can currently produce." That matters because it means the ceiling on near-term growth is a factory/supply-chain limit, not a sign that AI spending is cooling off.

2. Each new chip generation is worth more money per data center, not just more units

Think of a "gigawatt" as roughly one large data center's worth of power capacity. NVIDIA disclosed how much revenue it earns for every gigawatt of data center built with its chips, across three chip generations: about $18B with the old generation (Hopper), $25B with the current one (Blackwell), and an estimated $40B with the next one (Vera Rubin, starting to ship this quarter). Each new generation isn't just selling more chips into the same-size data center — it's earning more dollars for the exact same amount of physical infrastructure. That's a business getting more valuable per customer, not just bigger.

3. The growth is spreading beyond the handful of giant customers

Total data-center revenue grew 18% from the prior quarter, but revenue from NVIDIA's largest cloud customers (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta and similar) only grew 13%. That gap means the rest of NVIDIA's customer base — smaller specialized AI-compute providers, enterprises, and governments — is growing even faster than the giants. One concrete data point: smaller partners building data centers on NVIDIA's designs are on track to nearly triple their capacity this year (3GW to 8GW). This is a healthier growth pattern than depending on 3-4 mega-customers, since no single customer pulling back would hurt as much.

4. China is currently worth $0 to NVIDIA — and the reason is more interesting than "US export ban"

Two chip names matter here. H20 was a deliberately watered-down chip NVIDIA built specifically to comply with earlier US export rules — legal to sell to China, but weaker than NVIDIA's mainstream products. H200 is different: it's NVIDIA's actual full-power flagship chip (an upgraded H100, with more and faster memory), sold worldwide, not a China-specific cut-down variant.

The timeline: in April 2025, the US government blocked H20 exports to China entirely, costing NVIDIA a $4.5–5.5B charge on unsellable inventory. Then in December 2025, the US reversed course — it began allowing the full-power H200 (not just the watered-down H20) to ship to approved Chinese customers, on the condition that 25% of that China revenue goes to the US government. NVIDIA actually received its export license under this arrangement in February 2026. So by this quarter, the US side of the door was open.

The surprise is who closed it from the other side: China's own government. Reporting describes Beijing telling Chinese tech companies to pause their H200 orders — even though the US now permits the sale — reportedly to protect the case for China's own domestic chipmakers (like Huawei) and to keep negotiating leverage in the broader US-China tech relationship, rather than accept access on Washington's terms (including that 25% cut). The result: NVIDIA sold literally nothing to China this quarter (down from a token ~$60M the quarter before, under an earlier limited license). Management said this cost roughly $8B of orders this quarter alone, against a Chinese AI-chip market it estimates at around $50B a year.

The important shift: this is no longer a simple "waiting on US policy" story. It's a two-sided unlock — the US has already opened its side (with a revenue-share tax attached), but China's own government is currently the one keeping the door shut on its companies. This quarter's strong beat happened with none of that resolved, so today's numbers don't depend on either side changing its mind. But it also means the eventual catalyst, if it comes, requires both Washington and Beijing to move, not just one.

Put together, these four points explain why the beat and the raise both happened at once: NVIDIA is supply-constrained (not demand-constrained), each new chip generation earns more per data center, growth is broadening beyond the handful of giant cloud customers, and none of it currently includes China. That's a meaningfully more resilient growth story than "one big number beat expectations."
财报电话会议透露的四点信息，用大白话解释了这次超预期和上调指引的原因。其一，英伟达目前不是"卖不出去"，而是"造不出来"——公司表示供应链最多能支撑约70%的增长，但客户需求比这更高，也就是说下季度1080亿美元的指引不是"客户想买多少"，而是"公司目前最多能生产多少"，这说明近期增长的天花板来自产能限制，而非AI支出降温。其二，每一代新芯片在同样规模的数据中心里能赚更多钱：旧一代（Hopper）每吉瓦发电能力对应约180亿美元收入，当前一代（Blackwell）约250亿美元，下一代（Vera Rubin，本季度开始出货）预计约400亿美元——不是简单地卖更多芯片，而是同样的基础设施规模能创造更多收入。其三，增长正在从少数几个巨头客户向外扩散：整体数据中心收入环比增长18%，但来自最大几家云计算客户的收入只增长13%，说明其余客户（较小的专业AI算力提供商、企业、政府）增长更快；一个具体数据是，使用英伟达设计方案的中小型合作伙伴的产能今年将从3吉瓦增至8吉瓦，接近三倍——依赖客户更分散，比只依赖三四个巨头更健康。其四，中国市场目前对英伟达贡献为零，且背后是美中双方各自的政策博弈，详见上文。综合来看，这四点解释了为何这次能同时实现超预期和上调指引：供应不足而非需求不足、每代芯片单位经济效益提升、客户基础从巨头向外扩散、且完全未计入中国市场——这是比"单一数字超预期"更具韧性的增长故事。

Item 1 — The Bet: how big a move was priced in?

What it measures, plainly. Options traders were effectively taking a position on how far the stock would move the day after the report — up or down, direction not specified. That expected size is the "implied move." A 5.74% implied move means the market was pricing in a swing of roughly that much in either direction.

What happened. The expected move stayed in a narrow 5.7–6.4% band all week, drifting slightly down mid-week before ticking back up to 5.74% on the morning of the print. Throughout, that was about 1.8–2.0x NVDA's own typical earnings-day move (3.2%) — so the market was consistently pricing this as a bigger-than-usual event, without ever getting dramatic about it.

How it held up — and why there are two answers. The stock jumped 8.74% the next day. Against 5.74% priced in, that is a ratio of about 1.52x — the options were too cheap, and anyone who bought volatility going in was rewarded. But measured the way this project's pipeline does it — all the way out to the expiry date of the option contract being quoted (Aug 28) rather than just the next day — the move was only +3.76%, a ratio of about 0.66x, meaning the options were actually too expensive. Both numbers are correctly calculated; they answer different questions ("how did it react the next day" vs. "how did it do across the exact window the contract covered"). The entire difference is the Aug 28 pullback, which was mostly a sector-wide move rather than anything about NVDA's results.

The practical lesson from Item 1: whether the options market "got it right" depends entirely on which day you check the scoreboard. A single day's close is not the whole verdict.

Item 2 — The Premium: how much extra were people paying just because earnings were coming?

What it measures, plainly. Compare two groups of options on the same stock: those expiring right after the earnings date ("front"), and those expiring later, well clear of it ("back"). Both carry a price tag reflecting expected movement (implied volatility). The back-month options price in ordinary, everyday movement. The front-month ones price in ordinary movement plus the earnings event. The gap between them is, in effect, the price of the earnings event by itself.

What happened. The gap widened relentlessly, from 9.5 points to 35.7 points — nearly quadrupling. Front-month volatility climbed from 50.2% to 77.7% while back-month volatility barely moved at all (40.7% to 42.0%). The single largest jump (+9.1 points) landed on the morning of the print itself. Note what this means mechanically: the dollar cost of an earnings bet kept rising all week even while the expected percentage move from Item 1 was drifting slightly down.

How it held up. This was the most reliable signal of the week — the only one that moved in the same direction every single session, with no head-fakes. It correctly flagged "an unusually large event is coming" well before anyone could know the direction. Its one limitation is inherent: it says nothing at all about which way, and it still ended up understating how large the move would be.

Item 3 — The Lean: were traders paying up for upside or for protection?

What it measures, plainly. This compares the price of a moderately optimistic bet against a moderately pessimistic one, to see which side the crowd is paying up for. To keep it a fair comparison, both options are chosen to be roughly equally unlikely to pay off — about a 25% chance each — just in opposite directions. That is what "25-delta" means. Reading it as a directional forecast in real time would have meant reversing your view twice in three days. It only looks prescient with the benefit of knowing the ending.

Item 4 — The Footprint: what were traders actually doing, not just paying?

What it measures, plainly. Items 1–3 are all about prices. This one is about activity — how many put contracts changed hands relative to call contracts. The key reference point is 1.0: at 1.0, puts and calls trade in equal numbers. Below 1.0 means calls outnumber puts; above 1.0 means puts outnumber calls. A reading of 0.59 means roughly 59 puts traded for every 100 calls.

What happened. Every single reading, on both measures, sat below 1.0 — calls outnumbered puts throughout, never once close to parity. At the extreme (Aug 19) calls outnumbered puts more than three to one; at the most balanced point (Aug 22–23) still roughly three to two. Volume built from 0.29 to a plateau near 0.66, dipped to 0.38 on Aug 25, then finished at 0.59 on the morning of the print. Open interest told a quieter version of the same story, drifting from 0.38 up to 0.53 and back to 0.44.

How it held up. The persistent call-heavy skew of the flow is genuinely consistent with a bullish lean, and it did line up with the direction the stock ultimately went. But two honest caveats keep this from being a strong signal. First, the shape within the week was uninformative — it wandered up, down and back with no trend, and the tempting "calm confidence" read of the Aug 25 dip was undone by the very next reading. Second, and more important: large-cap US equities, and megacap tech especially, typically trade more calls than puts as a matter of course. Without NVDA's own longer-run baseline to compare against, a sub-1.0 ratio on its own does not establish that positioning was unusually bullish — only that it was call-tilted, as it usually is. The direction of the read is right; the strength of the evidence behind it is modest.

Scoring the four, honestly. Item 2 (the premium) was the standout — a clean, monotonic, no-head-fake warning that a big event was coming, though silent on direction. Item 1 (the bet) was directionally useful but its verdict flips depending on the measurement window. Item 3 (the lean) ended up right but oscillated too much to have been trustworthy live. Item 4 (the footprint) said essentially nothing this time. Taken together they described the magnitude of what was coming well and the direction of it barely at all — which is roughly what options positioning should be expected to do.

其一"押注"：衡量期权市场预期财报次日股价的波动幅度（不区分方向）。全周维持在5.7%-6.4%的窄幅区间，财报当日上午为5.74%，约为英伟达自身财报日典型波动（3.2%）的1.8-2.0倍——即市场始终将其定价为"大于平常但不夸张"的事件。结果如何：次日实际波动8.74%，是隐含值的约1.52倍，说明期权定价偏便宜；但若按本项目流水线的口径（计算至期权合约到期日8月28日，而非仅次日），实际波动仅3.76%，约为隐含值的0.66倍，反而说明期权偏贵。两个数字都算得正确，只是回答了不同的问题；两者差异几乎全部来自8月28日的回落，而该回落主要是板块性因素。

其二"溢价"：比较"财报后即到期"与"更晚到期"两类期权的定价差。远月期权只反映日常波动，近月期权则反映日常波动加上财报事件，两者之差即市场为财报事件本身支付的价格。该价差连续八晚扩大，从9.5个百分点升至35.7个百分点，近四倍；近月隐含波动率从50.2%升至77.7%，远月几乎不动（40.7%至42.0%），最大单日跳升（+9.1个百分点）恰好发生在财报当天上午。这也解释了为何期权的美元成本持续上升，而"其一"中的百分比预期反而略降。表现评价：这是全周最可靠的信号，唯一一个每个交易日都朝同一方向变动、没有反复的指标，提前明确提示"将有异常大的事件"，但它本质上无法提示方向，且仍低估了最终波动幅度。

其三"方向倾向"：比较"适度看涨"与"适度看跌"两种押注的价格，看市场更愿为哪一侧付费。为保证可比，两者被选为各自约25%的兑现概率（即"25Δ"）。计算方式恒为"看跌隐含波动率减看涨隐含波动率"，因此负值代表看涨一侧更贵。本周走势：连续五晚为负（看涨更贵），8月24日翻转为+2.19（看跌更贵），次日又翻回，财报当日上午收于−0.009，仍偏看涨但已接近中性。表现评价：最终方向正确，但因中途两次反复且最终接近零，是四项中最弱的信号——实时依赖它做方向判断意味着三天内两次调头。

其四"足迹"：前三项衡量的都是"价格"，这一项衡量"成交活动"——看跌期权成交量相对看涨期权的比例。0.59即每100张看涨期权约对应59张看跌期权，数值越高代表防御性交易相对越多。本周从0.29升至0.65-0.66的平台，8月25日骤降至0.38，财报当日上午回升至0.59，接近全周均值。表现评价：这一项这次几乎没有提供有效信息——8月25日的低点看似暗示"财报前情绪平稳自信"，但次日读数即推翻该解读，最终数值平淡无奇，更像是常规的双向持仓，而非方向性信号。

四项综合评分：其二（溢价）表现最佳，趋势干净、无反复，提前预警事件规模，但不指示方向；其一（押注）有参考价值，但结论随测量窗口而变；其三（方向倾向）最终正确却摆动过大，实时不可依赖；其四（足迹）本次基本无信息量。整体而言，四项指标较好地刻画了"幅度"，而对"方向"几乎无预测力——这大致也正是期权仓位数据应有的表现。

Consensus vs. Whisper, for This Print Specifically

Published consensus ($2.09 EPS) was cleared by 6.16% — but for a stock that beats nearly every quarter, "did it beat consensus" was never the real question. The more useful one is whether it beat the market's real, unstated expectation (the "whisper number"), which usually sits above the published figure. This project's framework for estimating the whisper gap without seeing it directly uses three tells: the pre-print run-up, the options-implied move, and the trend in recent beat magnitude. Unusually, all three point the same direction for this print — and it's a different direction than in most of this project's other coverage this earnings season.

Tell 1: the run-up. A stock that runs up hard into its print is telling you the market has quietly bid the whisper number well above consensus — raising the bar the actual results then have to clear. NVDA did the opposite: it walked in down 6.2% from its recent peak, on top of a broader sector selloff. That's not what a crowded, whisper-inflated setup looks like — if anything it suggests the market had gotten more cautious than consensus, not more excited.

Tell 2: the implied move. If the whisper number were running well ahead of consensus, you'd typically expect options positioning to reflect growing conviction as the print approached — a rising implied move, not a softening one. Instead the implied move eased, from a mid-week peak of 6.38% (Aug 20) down to 5.69% by Aug 25 and 5.74% on the morning of the print. The drift wasn't perfectly monotonic — it ticked up fractionally on the last read — but the direction across the week is clear: the market ended up pricing a slightly smaller expected swing than it had days earlier, consistent with expectations cooling rather than inflating.

Tell 3: the beat-range trend. The four quarters immediately before this print had compressed into a tight, unexciting 3.5–5.5% beat range — a stock the market had largely stopped expecting real surprises from. A well-behaved recent beat history is itself a sign the whisper number and consensus had converged, leaving little room for "everyone secretly expects more than this." This is the weakest of the three tells, though: four quarters is a short window, and NVDA had posted 8%-plus beats as recently as five quarters earlier, so the compression was a recent tendency rather than a firmly established pattern.

All three tells point to a whisper number that was likely close to, or even below, published consensus — the opposite setup from a name running hot into its print. That's a plausible explanation for why the reaction was so clean: a 6.16% beat that broke a six-quarter pattern landed as a genuine surprise against a lowered bar, rather than "expected, already priced in" against an inflated one. This is the first name in this project's coverage this earnings season where the run-up, the options positioning, and the beat-rate trend all lined up in the same (favorable) direction going into the print, instead of setting up a trap the way a hot run-up followed by decelerating guidance has elsewhere.
公开一致预期（每股收益2.09美元）被超预期6.16%——但对于一只几乎每季度都超预期的股票，"是否超过一致预期"从来不是真正的问题，更有意义的问题是它是否超过了市场真实但未公开的心理预期（即"whisper预期"，通常高于公开一致预期）。本项目用三个间接信号来估算这一预期缺口：财报前的抢跑涨幅、期权隐含波动率、以及近期超预期幅度的变化趋势。这次三个信号罕见地指向同一个方向，且与本项目本季度覆盖的其他大多数案例方向相反。信号一：抢跑涨幅——若股票在财报前大幅上涨，通常说明市场已经悄悄将whisper预期推高到远超公开一致预期的水平，抬高了实际业绩需要跨越的门槛；而英伟达这次恰恰相反，财报前较近期高点下跌6.2%，叠加板块整体走弱，这并非拥挤、whisper预期虚高的典型局面，反而说明市场比一致预期更谨慎，而非更亢奋。信号二：隐含波动率——如果whisper预期明显高于一致预期，通常会看到期权定价随财报临近而信心增强、隐含波动率上升；但实际情况相反，隐含单日波动预期从8月20日的高点6.38%回落至8月25日的5.69%、财报当日上午的5.74%。虽非严格单调下行（最后一次读数略有回升），但全周方向明确：市场最终定价的波动幅度小于数日之前，说明预期在财报前是降温而非升温。信号三：超预期幅度趋势——英伟达过去六个季度的超预期幅度收窄在3%-6%的狭窄区间内，市场已不再期待明显的意外，无论方向。超预期幅度趋于收窄、趋于"平淡"，本身就说明whisper预期与公开一致预期已经趋同，几乎没有"其实大家私下预期更高"的空间。综合三个信号：这次的whisper预期很可能接近甚至低于公开一致预期，与财报前抢跑上涨的典型局面相反。这可以解释为何这次市场反应如此干净——6.16%的超预期打破了六个季度的模式，是相对于一个被下调而非被推高的门槛而言的真正意外，而非"早已被消化"。这是本项目本季度覆盖中，抢跑涨幅、期权仓位与超预期趋势三者首次同时指向同一（正面）方向，而非像其他案例那样——抢跑上涨叠加指引减速，形成一个陷阱。

What This Is and Isn't

  • Covers the setup, the reported results, and three sessions of reaction (t0 Aug 27, t+1 Aug 28, t+2 Aug 31) — not a full multi-day drift study. Extended post-print drift (t+5, t+10) isn't in scope here; the base-rate comparison to the May print uses that print's own extended drift only as context, not as a claim about what happens next this time.
  • The May 20, 2026 print is cited as a base-rate caution, not analyzed in detail here.
  • "NVDA-specific" uses a simple one-index (SOXX) decomposition, not a rigorous factor model, and SOXX itself holds NVDA as a large constituent, so the sector figures may already be partly a read-through of NVDA's own move.
  • The guidance/margin commentary content of the actual release (beyond the headline revenue guide) isn't independently verified here beyond what's cited from external reporting.
  • Revenue and guidance figures are drawn from external news coverage published the day of the print, not from this project's own automated pipeline — the pipeline's own revenue-surprise field for this record was still catching up to yfinance's slower-updating fundamentals data as of this report.
  • The +3.76% expiry-window figure (and its 0.66x ratio) is now the value this project's own tracking pipeline holds for this print, captured on Aug 31 once its expiry-plus-buffer gate opened. An earlier version of that stored value was wrong: the pipeline was picking the wrong session as the "pre-earnings" reference for after-close prints like this one, inflating the figure to 7.01%. That was fixed and the stored record corrected before this version of the report.
