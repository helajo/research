Pre-Step
AI Research Bias Self-Check
Information richness: Grade A — abundant

Alphabet has been public for over 20 years, carries dense sell-side coverage (34–64+ analysts), and is one of the most-written-about companies in the world. Every angle of the bull and bear case has already been argued publicly, repeatedly.

AI research trap: with this much consensus material available, an AI-generated analysis tends to converge on the same narrative the market has already priced in — "search moat is deep," "AI transition is ahead of peers," "Cloud is compounding." All true, and already reflected in a $4.3T valuation. The alpha in reproducing consensus is roughly zero.

Mitigation used in this report: weighted deliberately toward inversion — what would have to be true for a smart investor to pass or short this name; what changed materially in the four weeks since the prior version of this report (2026-06-23); and where the two governing antitrust cases remain genuinely unresolved rather than "already decided," as casual coverage sometimes implies.

  • Does "certainty" come from data volume or business quality? Data volume is very high; this report treats the two as separate axes, not proxies for each other.
  • Does the output resemble sell-side consensus? Yes, largely — on most dimensions the facts genuinely support it. Divergence is flagged explicitly (ad-tech case, buyback pause, FY2026 FCF trough).
  • Is there any actual information edge? Essentially none. This report's value, if any, is rigor and even-handedness — not original discovery.

Step 2 · Duan Yongping Lens
Nature of the Business — "The Right Business"
Alphabet monetizes the world's largest pool of human attention (Search, YouTube, Android, Gmail) by selling access to it as precisely-targeted advertising, and is now attempting to re-platform that same attention pool — plus a second, enterprise-facing business (Cloud) — around generative AI.
  • Core engine — advertising (~76% of FY2025 revenue): classic two-sided platform flywheel — more users → more advertiser demand → more first-party data → better targeting → more users. Still intact, still the dominant profit source.
  • Growth engine — Google Cloud (~14.6% of revenue, +35.8% YoY FY25, +63% Q1'26): backlog nearly doubled QoQ to >$460B in Q1 2026 — a large forward signal, though conversion timing and margin at this scale remain unproven.
  • Frontier/defensive engine — Gemini (900M MAU, May 2026, up from 400M a year prior): both a defense of Search (AI Overviews embedded) and a new surface competing directly with ChatGPT.
  • Option asset — Waymo ($126B post-money, Feb 2026): real operating scale (~400–450K paid rides/week, 6 US metros) but still capital-consumptive, sitting inside a segment ("Other Bets") that has lost money every year in this dataset.

Gross margin expanded 55.4%→59.7% (2022–2025); operating margin 26.5%→32.0% over the same period — genuine operating leverage. The counter-fact that must sit next to this: FY2025 capex was $91.4B (+74% YoY), FY2026 guidance is $180–190B — roughly 1.4× FY2025 net income. Free cash flow fell from $73.3B (FY2025 full year) to just $10.1B in Q1 2026 alone. If unit economics are structurally improving, this capex eventually shows up as revenue/FCF growth; if it's a defensive arms race with lower returns, the last five years of operating leverage may not extrapolate. The data as of Q1 2026 supports both readings and does not yet resolve which is correct.

Duan Yongping's question
这门生意好在哪，一句话是什么？What's good about this business — and is it widening or narrowing?

Alphabet controls the largest attention funnel ever built and monetizes it near zero marginal cost per query. Search revenue is growing faster than it has in years (+19% YoY, Q1 2026) — arguing against near-term narrowing — but the mechanism (AI Overviews driving more queries, not necessarily more ad clicks per query) differs from the prior two decades' mechanism, and hasn't been tested through a full cycle.

中文摘要
一句话定义：Alphabet 通过搜索、YouTube、安卓等产品垄断全球最大的注意力池，将其变现为精准广告，目前正尝试用生成式AI重新平台化这一注意力池，并拓展企业云业务作为第二增长引擎。广告业务（约占FY2025营收76%）飞轮效应仍然完整；云业务（占比约14.6%，Q1 2026同比+63%）积压订单环比翻倍至4600亿美元以上；Gemini月活跃用户达9亿（一年前为4亿）；Waymo估值1260亿美元但仍处于烧钱阶段。毛利率从55.4%升至59.7%，经营杠杆显著——但2026年资本支出指引（1800-1900亿美元）约为净利润的1.4倍，这笔投入究竟是创造性投资还是防御性军备竞赛，目前数据无法判断。

Past 5 years: widened. Cloud went from persistent losses to full-year profitability; Gemini shipped at genuine scale (900M MAU); YouTube added a real subscription layer.

Next 5 years — search is genuinely two-sided in the data:

Widening case
Search & Other revenue +19% YoY (Q1'26, fastest in years), paid clicks +13%, CPC +5% — AI Overviews appear to be adding query volume without cannibalizing ad-serving so far. Google still gets 87.6% of referral traffic among clicks that leave search (Cloudflare Radar); the whole AI-chatbot cohort combined sends only 0.29%.
Narrowing case
SparkToro/Datos: 68% of Google searches now end with zero clicks (rising). Publisher-side traffic from Google down ~22% YoY in aggregate; small publishers (<10K daily pageviews) down ~60% over two years. Global search share eroding at its fastest YoY pace since 2009.

Both data sets are true simultaneously and describe different things: Google's share of the (shrinking) referral pie is intact; the pie itself, and the publisher ecosystem downstream, is shrinking. Whether that feeds back into Google's own ad economics is not yet resolved.

Buffett's question
10年后这条护城河还在吗？Will this moat still be here in 10 years? What could destroy it?

More likely to change form than be destroyed — from "ads on a results page" to "recommendations in an AI conversation." The real threat: a device maker (Apple, most obviously) replacing Google as the default retrieval surface, combined with DOJ remedies that now cap any default-placement deal (including for Gemini) at one year with forced annual re-bidding — already in effect since the Sept 2025 ruling (finalized Dec 2025).

Historical analogy — Microsoft in the 2000s: dominant, cash-generative incumbent facing simultaneous antitrust pressure and a paradigm shift (mobile then, AI now). Microsoft's resolution: mild antitrust outcome + a "lost decade" in mobile, re-rated a decade later once Azure proved out. The difference: Alphabet is not a laggard in AI — it originated the Transformer architecture and owns DeepMind; its risk is closer to "self-cannibalizing a highly profitable legacy business" than "missing the shift entirely."

Bear case, stated plainly:

  • Not cheap on the metric that matters: FY2025 GAAP P/E of 32.98x is above both the 10-year (~27x) and 5-year (~24-26x) average; the commonly-cited "TTM P/E ~27x" understates this due to a one-off equity gain.
  • Two antitrust cases genuinely open: search case on appeal both directions; ad-tech remedies ruling outstanding, could force AdX/DFP divestiture — less press attention, comparable structural risk.
  • FCF already visibly deteriorated (−47% YoY, Q1'26) ahead of the full FY2026 capex spend, with buybacks paused entirely the same quarter.
  • Two marquee AI researchers left for direct competitors in the same month — hard to reconcile with a pure "Google is winning the AI talent war" narrative.
Munger's question
我最可能在哪里犯错？Where am I most likely to be wrong?

Most likely error: under-weighting the ad-tech case. Coverage of "Google's antitrust risk" overwhelmingly focuses on the search case (already a relatively favorable no-breakup district-court outcome). The ad-tech case is a second, independent proceeding where liability is already established and the remedy — a forced AdX divestiture — remains undecided. The market's relief over "Chrome survived" may not price in a case most retail-facing coverage barely mentions.

Duan Yongping's question
如果CEO退休，公司还能保持竞争力吗？If Pichai retired tomorrow, would this business remain competitive?

Most likely yes — the competitive position rests on systemic, accumulated technical assets more than any single executive's irreplaceable judgment. The people whose departure would matter more are researchers like Demis Hassabis (still at Google) — and June 2026's Shazeer/Jumper exits show that kind of loss is not merely hypothetical.

Li Lu's question
20年后回看，这是标准石油还是昙花一现的3Com？Standard Oil of the AI era, or a passing 3Com?

Data supports "more likely the former" with more confidence than the reverse — full-stack positioning, three durable consumer-scale assets, financial capacity to fund decades-long bets without external financing pressure. The more precise risk isn't disappearance but transition from "high-margin growth compounder" to "large, slower-growing utility-like franchise" — closer to AT&T's fate than 3Com's.

Simulated commentary — four lenses

Business-model lens — Duan Yongping
This remains one of the best businesses ever built — a genuinely global, near-zero-marginal-cost distribution of human attention. What should give any owner pause is not the business's quality but its transformation: a company that used to spend lightly and print cash is now spending $180-190B a year, more than its own net income, to defend and extend that position. Good businesses do not usually need to spend at that rate simply to stay good — either this capex is building something durably better, or it is buying time. The data available today does not yet tell us which.
Inversion lens — Munger
Invert — under what conditions does this investment lose money? Not through a technology failure, and not through obvious management incompetence. The more plausible failure paths are structural and legal: a D.C. Circuit reversal reinstating the Chrome/Android breakup, or a forced AdX divestiture in a second antitrust case that gets a fraction of the attention the search case receives. Both are live, both are unresolved, and neither shows up in the "TTM P/E ~27x, cheap relative to Mag7" framing that dominates casual coverage.
Management & values lens — Buffett + Duan Yongping
Pichai's best decision on record is arguably the 2019 choice to keep DeepMind inside Google — a choice that now underwrites Gemini's 900M-user product. But the dual-class structure giving Page and Brin 52.7% of votes against 6.2% of economic ownership remains the least "shareholder is buying a person you'd trust with unchecked power" governance arrangement in this coverage universe — it is not a hypothetical risk, it is the literal legal structure of the company today.
Civilizational lens — Li Lu
Twenty years from now, Alphabet most likely remains one of the handful of companies that mattered in the AI transition — it controls three durable digital-era assets that AI is reshaping in form but has not yet displaced in substance. The open question worth revisiting every year, not answering once: is the current capex the price of staying the standard-bearer of this era, or is it a tell that the standard-bearer already senses the ground shifting under it?
