SECTION 1 · 第一节
Company Background & Business Overview
公司背景与业务概述
ASML Holding N.V. was founded in 1984 as a joint venture between Philips and ASM International, originally known as ASM Lithography. Headquartered in Veldhoven, the Netherlands, it went public on NASDAQ in 1995 and rebranded to its current name in 2001. Today, ASML is arguably the most strategically important company in the global semiconductor supply chain — not because of its size, but because it makes machines that no other company on earth can replicate.
ASML's core product is the lithography system: a machine that uses light to etch circuit patterns onto silicon wafers, the fundamental step in making every modern chip. The company is the world's sole supplier of Extreme Ultraviolet (EUV) lithography systems, which use light at a 13.5 nanometre wavelength to print the ultra-fine patterns required for the most advanced chips (below 7nm). Without EUV, companies like TSMC, Samsung, and Intel cannot manufacture leading-edge chips. ASML also supplies Deep Ultraviolet (DUV) systems — both dry and immersion — for older technology nodes, and sells metrology and inspection systems through its HMI brand. Its Installed Base Management (IBM) segment generates recurring revenue through service contracts, spare parts, and system upgrades.
As of Q1 2026, ASML employed 43,882 people globally FMP/SEC. Its customers are exclusively the world's leading chipmakers: TSMC, Samsung, Intel, SK Hynix, Micron, and others. Revenue is geographically diverse — Taiwan, South Korea, Europe, and the US are major contributors — though China has historically been a significant market that is now under active restriction. CEO Christophe Fouquet, who took over in 2024, has maintained the company's long-horizon investment focus and guided confidently despite the geopolitical headwinds.
ASML's competitive moat is among the deepest of any publicly traded company. EUV systems require the integration of over 100,000 precision components, including the world's most powerful lasers, mirrors accurate to the atomic level, and vacuum systems of extraordinary complexity. The company has spent over three decades and tens of billions of euros building this capability. The moat is reinforced by customer lock-in (systems are deeply embedded in fab processes), a massive installed base that generates service revenue, and a next-generation product pipeline through High-NA EUV — systems capable of printing even smaller features for sub-2nm chips.

ASML maintains a fortress balance sheet. The company ended 2025 with €12.9 billion in cash and short-term investments, virtually no net debt in the traditional sense, and shareholders' equity representing 43.3% of total assets as of Q1 2026. The company's most strategically important balance sheet item is arguably its backlog: at €38.8 billion as of year-end 2025, it represents approximately 1.2 times full-year revenue and provides exceptional multi-year revenue visibility. This backlog is largely composed of EUV and High-NA system orders from TSMC, Samsung, Intel, and memory makers, making it extremely sticky. ASML returns substantial capital to shareholders — in 2025 it paid out €8.7 billion through dividends and buybacks, and raised the 2025 dividend by 17% to €7.50 per ordinary share. The company's R&D investment is also notable at €4.7 billion in 2025, roughly 14% of revenue, funding the High-NA roadmap and next-generation metrology tools.

ASML occupies a truly unique position within semiconductor equipment. While Applied Materials (AMAT), Lam Research (LRCX), and KLA (KLAC) collectively cover deposition, etch, and process control, none of them competes in lithography at the leading edge — the step that determines which circuit patterns get printed. Nikon was the only historical competitor in DUV lithography, but failed to develop a commercially viable EUV system, ceding the field entirely to ASML. This makes ASML less a "peer" of other semicon equipment companies and more akin to a utility — every advanced chip fab must buy from ASML, and there is no alternative supplier. This dynamic justifies a premium multiple relative to peers, though it also attracts geopolitical scrutiny, as both the US and Chinese governments understand the strategic leverage ASML holds.

SECTION 6 · 第六节
Growth Catalysts & Competitive Moat
增长催化剂与竞争护城河
High-NA EUV: ASML has begun shipping its next-generation High Numerical Aperture (High-NA) EUV system, the TWINSCAN EXE:5000. By Q1 2025, ASML had shipped its fifth High-NA system to three different customers. These machines are significantly more expensive than current Low-NA EUV systems (reportedly over €350 million each) and are required for sub-2nm chip manufacturing. Intel, TSMC, and Samsung are all receiving or evaluating High-NA systems. Production ramp targets include at least 60 EUV Low-NA systems in 2026 and a path to 80+ by 2027, alongside growing High-NA shipments.
AI Infrastructure Demand: The surge in AI chip demand — led by NVIDIA, AMD, custom silicon from hyperscalers (Google TPU, Amazon Trainium, Microsoft Maia) — is driving direct demand for ASML's most advanced EUV systems. As AI models grow in size and complexity, the demand for TSMC's and Samsung's most advanced nodes (3nm, 2nm, and beyond) drives equipment orders. CEO Fouquet has consistently cited AI as "the primary growth driver in our industry."
Memory Recovery: ASML generated €8.4 billion in memory system revenue in 2025 — a notable recovery driven by HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) demand for AI accelerators. SK Hynix, Micron, and Samsung are all investing heavily in HBM3E capacity. Memory chips require lithography at increasingly advanced nodes, supporting continued ASML tool demand.
2030 Investor Day Vision: At its 2024 Investor Day, ASML modelled a revenue opportunity of €44–60 billion by 2030, with gross margin expanding to 56–60%. This long-run target is underpinned by High-NA adoption, memory growth, and continued AI capex by hyperscalers and chip designers.
Moat depth: The competitive moat comprises: (1) sole-source EUV supply — no alternative exists; (2) 40+ years of lithography IP and manufacturing know-how; (3) a vast installed base creating sticky service revenue; (4) a €38.8B backlog providing multi-year revenue certainty; and (5) High-NA as a future revenue step-up.

China Revenue Headwind

Tariff & Trade Uncertainty
Trump-era tariff volatility has increased macro uncertainty. While ASML's machines themselves are not directly tariffed, customer capex decisions are sensitive to macro conditions. A US-Europe trade deal could be a positive catalyst.

Semiconductor Cycle Position
The non-AI chip segment (consumer electronics, automotive, industrial) remains in a recovery phase, while AI-driven logic chips are in a full upcycle. ASML benefits disproportionately from the AI segment but is not immune to non-AI weakness.
半导体周期位置：AI逻辑芯片处于强劲上行周期，而消费电子、汽车及工业类非AI芯片仍在复苏阶段，ASML受益于AI上行但并非完全免疫非AI领域的疲软。
The semiconductor equipment sector is in a bifurcated cycle: AI-driven leading-edge investment is booming, while legacy node capex remains subdued. ASML sits firmly in the AI beneficiary camp for its most profitable EUV product line, but its DUV business faces dual headwinds — Chinese restrictions and lower legacy demand. The industry is also increasingly shaped by geopolitics: both the US and Netherlands have imposed export control regimes, and the proposed MATCH Act in the US could extend restrictions even to older DUV immersion tools. The rare earth dispute between China and the US (suspended until November 2026 as part of a trade deal) adds a supply chain risk layer.

SECTION 8 · 第八节
Geopolitical, Macro & Regulatory Risk
地缘政治、宏观与监管风险
🇨🇳 China Export Controls
China's share of ASML's revenue has collapsed from ~49% in 2023 to ~19% in Q1 2026. Further tightening — especially if the MATCH Act passes or Dutch authorities widen DUV immersion restrictions — could eliminate what remains of China DUV sales. ASML has said the impact of known restrictions falls within guidance, but future escalation is unpredictable.
🇺🇸🇳🇱 US-Netherlands Policy Alignment
The Netherlands government follows but does not always move in lockstep with US restrictions. The Affiliates Rule introduced in October 2025 was suspended for one year (until Nov 2026) as part of a US-China trade deal. Its eventual implementation or lapse is a key watchpoint.
⚗️ Rare Earth Supply Chain
China suspended export restrictions on rare earth minerals until November 2026 as part of a trade agreement. ASML's precision components may have rare earth dependencies. If restrictions resume post-November 2026, supply chain disruption risk rises.
💱 EUR/USD Currency Risk
ASML reports in euros but trades as a USD ADR. A strengthening euro (or weakening dollar) reduces the USD-translated value of earnings for ADR holders. With beta of 1.37, the stock is also more volatile than the broader market.
📦 Order Lumpiness
High-NA systems are recognised on milestone completion, creating quarter-to-quarter revenue volatility. Net bookings can swing dramatically (€3.9B in Q1 2025 vs €13.2B in Q4 2025), making near-term estimates difficult and creating stock price overreaction risk.
🔬 Technology Risk
While no credible EUV alternative exists today, a breakthrough in alternative lithography (e.g., direct e-beam patterning at scale, or a Chinese state-funded EUV programme) would be an extreme long-term risk. SMIC has made some DUV-based advances but remains multiple generations behind.

SECTION 11 · 第十一节
Practical Guidance for Retail Investors
零售投资者实用指引
Who this stock suits: ASML is a core holding for long-duration growth investors who want AI infrastructure exposure without the direct model/software risk. It suits investors with a 3–5+ year horizon who can tolerate significant volatility (beta 1.37, 52-week range of 2.3x). It is less suited to income investors (dividend yield ~0.5%) or short-term traders, as earnings recognition is lumpy and macro headlines can cause sharp moves.
Position sizing: Given the beta of 1.37, the China/geopolitical overhang, and the premium valuation, a satellite weighting of 2–5% in a diversified global equity portfolio is appropriate for most retail investors. For those with higher conviction on the AI infrastructure supercycle, a core position of up to 8% could be justified — but the China risk warrants a smaller starting position with room to add on pullbacks.

What to Monitor

  • Net Bookings per Quarter: This is the leading indicator. Below €5B/quarter would signal demand weakness; above €7B signals upcycle. Q4 2025 was €13.2B — exceptionally strong.
  • China Revenue %: Monitor whether the share stabilises at ~20% or continues declining toward zero. Tipping below 10% would indicate full restriction enforcement.
  • High-NA Shipment Count: Each system shipped and revenue milestone hit adds €350M+ to revenue recognition. Track quarterly shipment disclosures carefully.

Entry Considerations

The AI Infrastructure Stack framework maps the full AI value chain into eight layers — from physical silicon through to cloud application delivery. What This Layer Does · 该层的作用

Layer 1 — Silicon Brain — encompasses the tools and technologies that enable the physical creation of semiconductors: chip design EDA software, lithography equipment, and the manufacturing processes that print billions of transistors onto silicon wafers. Without Layer 1, there is no AI — no GPU, no ASIC, no chip of any kind exists.

Why ASML Belongs Here · 为何归属此层

ASML is the purest expression of Layer 1. It manufactures the lithography machines — particularly EUV systems — that are the essential tool for printing the most advanced semiconductor circuits. Every AI chip from NVIDIA, every Apple silicon, every Google TPU, every Amazon Trainium must pass through an ASML machine to be made. ASML does not design chips or write software; it makes the physical manufacturing equipment, placing it at the absolute foundation of the AI value chain. Peer semicon equipment companies (AMAT, LRCX, KLAC) also operate in Layer 1 but none hold ASML's EUV monopoly.
