Why Hold

1

The AI networking growth story is real — and already in the price

Arista's AI networking revenue guide doubled in a year ($1.5bn FY25A → $3.5bn FY26E target), and management's TAM has doubled to $105bn by 2029E. But at 19.6x EV/NTM-revenue, ANET already trades in line with our growth-adjusted "fair" multiple of 20.25x — the premium is earned, not excessive, but it leaves little room for further re-rating.

2

The best balance sheet and the longest-tenured team in the group

Zero debt, $12.3bn of cash, GAAP-profitable every year since before the 2014 IPO, one CEO since 2008. This lowers execution risk relative to newer AI-infrastructure names — it doesn't create upside from current levels on its own.

3

Three independent methods diverge sharply — and converge below today's price

DCF ($49–$79), growth-adjusted comps ($77–$197), and precedent transactions ($19–$49) — built separately, none reverse-engineered to a target — blend to $137 at 25/65/10 weights. Comps gets the most weight deliberately: DCF structurally understates a name whose value depends on growth well past a 5-year window.

4

Nvidia and customer concentration are the swing factors

Microsoft and Meta are ~40% of revenue. Nvidia's Spectrum-X has already overtaken Arista and Cisco combined in overall datacenter Ethernet switching share (IDC, 1Q26) — the single biggest threat to the open-Ethernet thesis this report is built on.

Sensitivity check

Even at the most bullish corner of our DCF sensitivity grid — 10.2% WACC and 4.0% terminal growth — fair value tops out at $79, still 55% below today's price. Closing that gap requires either a WACC below what a beta-1.6 name should carry, or a growth runway extending well past our 5-year explicit window. Read correctly: the market is pricing durable AI-networking share gains into the early 2030s, not just the next five years.

Risk / Catalyst Map

Biggest risks to the HOLD (upside)

  • Beat-and-raise continues. The $3.5bn FY26E AI networking target has already been raised once — a repeat would keep pushing estimates up.
  • Campus/VeloCloud outperforms. HPE-Juniper integration friction could hand Arista faster-than-modeled enterprise share.
  • Open Ethernet wins decisively. A major hyperscaler publicly standardizing away from Spectrum-X would remove the top competitive risk.
  • Premium multiples persist. Comparable compounders (PANW, FTNT) have held rich valuations for years — the DCF gap can close via growth, not just price.
  • Capital return accelerates. $12.3bn cash, zero debt — buybacks already stepped up 4x YoY in FY25A.

Watch next

  • Aug 5, 2026 — Q2 FY26 earnings. Next read on whether the raised AI networking guide holds.
  • Microsoft & Meta capex commentary. ~40% of revenue rides on these two budgets.
  • IDC quarterly Ethernet share tracker. Watch whether Nvidia's lead over Arista+Cisco widens or narrows.
  • Campus/VeloCloud disclosure. Any segment-level detail is the tell on the second growth engine.
  • Broadcom Tomahawk/Jericho roadmap. Arista's silicon cadence is not fully its own to control.
